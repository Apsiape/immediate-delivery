\section{Antisymmetric rigidity and purity accounting}\label{app:purity}

\subsection{A dimension-free local estimate}

\begin{lemma}[Antisymmetric support decouples the source purifier]\label{lem:qutrit-rigidity}
Let a finite unitary round have source $\beta$, channel $\Psi$, and
$\delta=d_J(\Psi,\Theta)$. Purify the source by $F$, feed a Bell pair
on $RA$, and denote the final $RYF$ marginal by $\chi$. Then
\[
 \|\chi-\cJ(\Theta)\otimes\beta_F\|_1\leq20\sqrt\delta,
 \qquad a\geq H-g_9(\min\{1,10\sqrt\delta\}).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose the antisymmetric isometry $W:\C^3\to RY$, sending the three
coordinate vectors to the normalized alternating pairs, with signs
chosen so its partial-trace channel is $\Theta$. Put $P=WW^*$.
Since $\cJ(\Theta)=P/3$, support loss
$\Tr((I-P)\chi)\leq\delta$. Gentle projection gives
$\|\chi-P\chi P\|_1\leq2\sqrt\delta$.
The untouched marginal is $\chi_{RF}=\tau_3\otimes\beta_F$.
Thus, for the subnormalized $\zeta=W^*P\chi PW$,
\[
 \|(\Theta\otimes\id_F)(\zeta)-\tau_3\otimes\beta_F\|_1
 \leq2\sqrt\delta.
\]
The inverse linear map is
$\Theta^{-1}(Y)=\Tr(Y)I-2Y^T$. The first summand has stabilized
trace norm $3$, and transposition on $M_3$ has stabilized trace norm
$3$, so the inverse has norm at most $9$. This also acts on
subnormalized and nonpositive differences. Since
$\Theta(\tau_3)=\tau_3$, it follows that
$\|\zeta-\tau_3\otimes\beta_F\|_1\leq18\sqrt\delta$.
Re-embedding by $W$ and adding the gentle error~\cite[Lemma~9]{Winter1999} proves the first claim.

The global state on $RYB'F$ is pure. Therefore the actual global
bath gain is
$S(B')-S(\beta)=H(RY|F)_\chi$. Conditional-entropy continuity
on the fixed dimension $\dim(RY)=9$ gives the second claim
\cite[Lemma~2]{Winter2016}; its comparison state has conditional
entropy $\log_2 3$. No bound on $\dim F$ is used.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exports, imports and exact banks}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:qutrit-purity}]
Send fresh Bell inputs and retain every output and reference.
Each round's marginal Choi error is at most $\epsilon$, even when
the memory is correlated with past outputs.
For an intra-round exchange schedule, place every prescribed independent
import at the mathematical beginning and retain every export to the
mathematical end. Extend each operation by identities on unused imports
and retired exports. This gives one finite unitary round with the same
reduced channel. It is bookkeeping, not a smaller-memory implementation.

Let active dimensions before and after be $b,b'$, the product import
dimension be $a_0$, and the product export dimension be $g$. Then
$ba_0=b'g$. If active marginals are $\eta,\eta'$, and the independent
import state is $\sigma$, subadditivity bounds the round's global gain by
\begin{align*}
 a_{\rm glob}
 &\leq S(\eta')+\log_2g-S(\eta)-S(\sigma)\\
 &=F(\eta)+c-F(\eta'),
 \quad F(\eta)=\log_2b-S(\eta),\quad
 c=\log_2a_0-S(\sigma).
\end{align*}
This uses only unconditional marginals and remains valid with past
correlations. Summing telescopes to $\sum_ta_{\rm glob,t}\leq P+C$,
because the final deficit is nonnegative. Each gain is nonnegative
for the reconstructed round with maximally mixed system input.
Lemma~\ref{lem:qutrit-rigidity} proves the lower, including its positive
part. Correlated imports would require their joint deficit to be charged;
adaptive state selection during a round is not included.

For the exact upper, let $b=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil$ and
$q=\lceil bH\rceil$. Encode $b$ pure qutrit Stinespring cells in
$q$ qubits. Apply the cell isometry at its round and never touch that cell
again until the bank is exported. For every full bank except the last,
export all $q$ qubits and import a pure bank. Unused cells in the final
bank stay pure; no accuracy error occurs. A reversible schedule clock
costs $\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$ pure qubits, and a fixed wire encoding
costs $O(1)$. With $B=\lceil n/b\rceil$,
\[
 Q=q+O(\log n),\quad P+C=Bq+O(\log n),\quad J=(B-1)q.
\]
Since $Bb=n+O(b)$ and $B=O(\sqrt n)$, these are the claimed ledgers.
Unitary extensions preserve the encoded bank and its unused padding.
They realize independent channels for every adaptive observer.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:qutrit-gap}]
Werner twirling fixes $\Theta$, contracts Choi trace distance and
preserves factorizability: each conjugation preserves it, and convex
mixtures and limits preserve the relevant closure. The twirled channel
is $T_\lambda=\lambda W_3^++(1-\lambda)W_3^-$. Its two normalized
Choi states occupy orthogonal symmetric and antisymmetric subspaces,
so $d_J(T_\lambda,\Theta)=\lambda$.
Haagerup--Musat's twirl calculation gives
$\lambda\geq2/27$ for every factorizable twirl, and an exact
factorization at equality through $M_3\otimes M_3$
\cite[Lemmas~5.4 and~5.5, Theorem~5.6 and Corollary~5.7]{HaagerupMusat2015}. The threshold persists under limits.
This proves both bounds for the closure $\overline{\mathcal F}$. The published
full cb distance is $4/27$ (half-diamond distance $2/27$); no generic conversion of that norm into
Choi distance is needed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A lower bound for delayed batches}

Suppose contiguous batches contain $b_n=o(n)$ qutrit inputs and release
their outputs together, with complete error at most $n^{-2}$.
Within and between batches allow the prescribed independent exchange
just accounted for. Define
\[
 K_n=\left\lfloor\frac{\log_2n-3\log_2\log_2(n+2)}{\log_2 9}
       \right\rfloor.
\]
The following assertion concerns sufficiently large $n$, so $K_n\geq1$.
\begin{proposition}[Batch probes]\label{prop:batch-purity-lower}
Every such service satisfies
\[
 \frac{P+C}{n}\geq (H-o(1))\min\{1,K_n/b_n\}.
\]
In particular $b_n=o(\log n)$ forces asymptotic supplied-purity
rate $H$, and the exact banks above attain it with $Q_{\rm peak}=o(n)$,
$J=O(n)$. For arbitrary $b_n$, if $R[b]$ is the infimum achievable
$\limsup(P+C)/n$ under these resource constraints and
$x=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\log_2n/b_n\in[0,\infty]$, then
\[
 R[b]\geq H\min\{1,x/\log_2 9\}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In a full batch probe $m=\min(b_n,K_n)$ inputs with Bell pairs and
set the others to $\tau_3$. Regard the unused inputs as an analytical
bath, and retain their outputs at the mathematical end. By subadditivity
and their maximum entropy, its gain is at most the actual bath gain:
\[
 S(B'Y_0)-S(B)-(b_n-m)H\leq S(B')-S(B).
\]
Tensor the alternating isometry $m$ times. Support loss is at most
$n^{-2}$, and the stabilized inverse bound is $9^m$. The proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:qutrit-rigidity} now gives half trace distance at most
$t_n=(1+9^m)/n$ from the decoupled target state. Consequently the
physical bath gain is at least $mH-g_{9^m}(t_n)$.
By the definition of $K_n$,
$t_n\leq n^{-1}+(\log_2(n+2))^{-3}$.
Since $\log_2(9^m)=m\log_2 9$,
$g_{9^m}(t_n)=o(m)$ uniformly for $1\leq m\leq K_n$.
The leftover batch has fewer than $b_n=o(n)$ rounds and nonnegative
global gain, so it can be dropped from the lower sum.
There are $\lfloor n/b_n\rfloor$ full batches. The deficit ledger
therefore gives the displayed bound. Write $z_n=\log_2n/b_n$. Since
$K_n/\log_2n\to1/\log_2 9$, the bounded factors obey
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\min\{1,K_n/b_n\}
 =\min\{1,(\limsup_{n\to\infty}z_n)/\log_2 9\}.
\]
For finite $\limsup z_n$ this follows by continuity and a subsequence
attaining the limsup; for infinite limsup the same subsequence makes
the capped expression tend to one. The uniform $o(1)$ and the
discarded fraction $b_n/n=o(1)$ vanish before taking the limsup.
The lower holds for every admissible service sequence and hence for
their infimum $R[b]$. This proves the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A counted collective Gaussian implementation}

The following finite matrix argument controls both the isometry and
the implemented channel. A small input Gram alone would not control
the channel's Kraus coefficient Gram.

\begin{lemma}[Rectangular Gaussian packing, extending~{\cite[Theorem~1]{DouglasSchur}}]\label{lem:collective-gaussian}
Let $\Psi$ be a unital trace-preserving channel on $M_D$ with $r$
Kraus operators. For $0<\delta\leq1/2$ it has a finite unitary
implementation of half-diamond error at most $\delta$ with a flat
rank-$k$ source in a dyadic $m$-dimensional bath, where
\begin{align*}
 \log_2 k&=\lceil\tfrac12\log_2r\rceil,\\
 \log_2m&\leq\tfrac12\log_2r+2\log_2(1/\delta)
       +2\log_2\bigl(1+\ln(8Dk)\bigr)+O(1).
\end{align*}
Its supplied purity is $\log_2(m/k)$; no random seed is supplied.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\Psi(X)=\sum_\alpha A_\alpha X A_\alpha^*$ and let
$G_\alpha$ be independent $m\times k$ complex Gaussian matrices
with entry variance $1/m$. Put $W=\sum_\alpha A_\alpha\otimes G_\alpha$,
$Z=W^*W-I$. We first justify the dimension-uniform moment estimate
\[
 \|\E Z^{2p}\|\leq(2p)!(k/m)^p,\qquad k\leq m.
\]
Wick expansion of a $q$th power and cancellation of fixed points
under centering give
\[
 \E Z^q=\sum_{\substack{\pi\in S_q\\\pi\text{ has no fixed point}}}
  m^{c(\pi)-q}k^{c(\gamma\pi)-1}T_\pi\otimes I_k,
 \qquad \gamma=(1\,2\,\cdots\,q).
\]
Here $c$ counts cycles. Row-index loops supply the $m$ powers,
column-index loops the $k$ powers; the one external column pair
accounts for the exponent minus one. Removing a fixed point
contracts $\sum_\alpha A_\alpha^*A_\alpha=I$ and leaves the other
loop factors unchanged, exactly matching the subtraction of $I$.
The contracted system word is
$T_\pi=\sum_{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_q}
 A_{\alpha_1}^*A_{\alpha_{\pi(1)}}\cdots
 A_{\alpha_q}^*A_{\alpha_{\pi(q)}}$.

Each $T_\pi$ is a contraction, including crossing pairings.
Read its word from right to left. At a label's first occurrence create
that label with either isometry
$v\mapsto\sum_\alpha A_\alpha v\otimes\ket\alpha$ or
$v\mapsto\sum_\alpha A_\alpha^*v\otimes\ket\alpha$.
Both are isometries, respectively by trace preservation and unitality.
At its second occurrence erase it by the adjoint of its creation map.
Permuting the open label registers is unitary. Each label appears
once starred and once unstarred, so this construction gives exactly
the contracted word and bounds its norm by one.
For a fixed-point-free permutation $c(\pi)\leq q/2$, and
$c(\pi)+c(\gamma\pi)\leq q+1$. The latter follows from the triangle
inequality for transposition length $|\sigma|=q-c(\sigma)$.
With $a=q-c(\pi)\geq q/2$ the coefficient is at most
$(k/m)^a\leq(k/m)^{q/2}$. There are at most $q!$ terms, proving
the moment bound.

Choose $k=2^{\lceil(\log_2r)/2\rceil}$,
$p=\lceil\ln(8Dk)\rceil$, $\eta=\delta/2$, and take $m$ to be
the least power of two at least $1024kp^2/\eta^2$.
Since $Z$ is Hermitian, trace Markov gives
\[
 \Pr\{\|Z\|>\eta\}\leq
 Dk\left(\frac{4p^2k}{m\eta^2}\right)^p<1/4.
\]
Also $C_{\alpha\beta}=k^{-1}\Tr(G_\alpha^*G_\beta)$ is the
covariance matrix of $mk$ independent standard Gaussian samples
divided by $mk$. For a fixed unit vector its quadratic form is the
mean of $mk$ exponential variables; exponential Markov bounds the
two tails. A $1/4$-net of the complex unit sphere, of size at most
$9^{2r}$, gives
\[
 \Pr\{\|C-I\|>\eta\}
 \leq2\,9^{2r}\exp(-mk\eta^2/32)<1/4.
\]
The chosen parameters suffice since $k^2\geq r$.
Hence one realization satisfies both bounds. Fix it as hardware.

Before normalization, tracing $W(X\otimes I_k/k)W^*$ implements
the Kraus coefficient matrix $C^T$. Its distance from $I$ is the
same as that of $C$. The coefficient perturbation has
diamond norm at most $\eta$: use the Stinespring isometry of $\Psi$
and trace duality with $C-I$. Polar normalization
$V=W(W^*W)^{-1/2}$ gives an isometry with
$\|V-W\|\leq\eta$, $\|W\|\leq\sqrt{1+\eta}$.
Left-right multiplication bounds its additional diamond error by
$3\eta$. The total half-diamond error is at most $2\eta=\delta$.
Complete $V:\C^D\otimes\C^k\to\C^D\otimes\C^m$ to a unitary
on the bath $\C^m$, initialized uniformly on the embedded $\C^k$.
This source has entropy $\log_2k$, giving the stated ledger.
All probabilities prove finite existence; neither normalization nor
Gaussian randomness is an online operation or postselection.
\end{proof}

For $\Theta^{\otimes b}$, $D=r=3^b$ exactly. Thus the lemma yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qutrit-collective-ledger}
\begin{aligned}
 Q_{{\rm bath},b}&\leq bH/2+2\log_2(1/\delta)+2\log_2(b+1)+O(1),\\
 P_b&\leq2\log_2(1/\delta)+2\log_2(b+1)+O(1).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The $b$ accepted system registers are held until the batch output;
they use at most $2b$ encoded qubits and are
counted in $Q_{\rm peak}$. They are observer payload, rather than
part of the fixed auxiliary source whose entropy deficit is $P_b$.
They leave as the system output, so their release does not add to
$J$. With $b=n$, the displayed bath ledger and this custody count
prove Theorem~\ref{thm:qutrit-collective}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:batch-purity}]
The lower is Proposition~\ref{prop:batch-purity-lower}; exact banks
give $R[b]\leq H$ for every allowed batch sequence.
For $b_n\geq\log_2n$, put $B=\lceil n/b_n\rceil$ and
$\delta=n^{-2}/B$. Renew the whole collective bath independently
between batches. The adaptive hybrid costs at most $B\delta=n^{-2}$,
since there are no retained bank correlations after its permanent
export. From~\eqref{eq:qutrit-collective-ledger} and $Bb_n\leq n+b_n$,
\begin{align*}
 P_b\leq4\log_2n+2\log_2B+2\log_2(b_n+1)+O(1)
       &\leq6\log_2n+O(1),\\
 Q_{{\rm bath},b}&\leq b_nH/2+O(\log n).
\end{align*}
Add a pure reversible clock of $O(\log n)$ qubits, charged once.
A shorter final batch uses its own smaller $k,m$ and flat inactive
padding in the common bank; that padding costs zero purity, not
an uncharged pure register. The payload bank can accept inputs in sequence and be transformed
in place by the collective unitary. Hence
$Q_{\rm peak}\leq(2+H/2)b_n+O(\log n)=o(n)$ and $J=O(n)$.
The supplied rate is at most
$6\log_2n/b_n+o(1)$ in this range. For smaller $b_n$ use exact banks;
the pointwise minimum with exact banks is
$\min\{H,6z_n\}+o(1)$ (when $z_n>1$, exact banks already
achieve the minimum). The same limsup identity gives
$R[b]\leq\min\{H,6x\}$, including $x=\infty$.
If $b_n/\log n\to\infty$, renewing the $O(\log n)$ overhead
$O(n/b_n)$ times costs $o(n)$ exchange, and the leftover batch
changes it by $O(b_n+\log n)=o(n)$. Hence $J=nH/2+o(n)$.
This last near-minimal exchange assertion is not made at critical
logarithmic batching. The lower is positive exactly when $x>0$. For the nonnegative
sequence $z_n$, $x=0$ is equivalent to $b_n/\log_2n\to\infty$,
which proves the zero-rate criterion. If $b_n=o(\log n)$ then
$x=\infty$, so the corrected lower is $H$ and matches exact banks.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:unital-batch}]
Write $z_n=\log_2n/b_n$, so $x=\limsup_nz_n$.

\emph{Exact banks.} Fix a Stinespring isometry of $\Psi$ with a pure
environment of dimension $r$. The banks in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:qutrit-purity}, with $r$-dimensional cells and
$q=\lceil\lceil\sqrt n\rceil\log_2r\rceil$, give exact services with
$Q=O(\sqrt n)$, $P+C=n\log_2r+O(\sqrt n)$ and $J=O(n)$. Holding each
output until its batch ends keeps the error zero and adds at most
$b_n\lceil\log_2d\rceil$ payload qubits to $Q_{\rm peak}$.

\emph{Collective batches.} Let $n\geq2$, $B=\lceil n/b_n\rceil$ and
$\delta=n^{-2}/B$. A batch of $b'\leq b_n$ inputs uses
Lemma~\ref{lem:collective-gaussian} for $\Psi^{\otimes b'}$, which is
unital with $D=d^{b'}$ and Choi rank $r^{b'}\leq d^{2b'}$. Thus
$k\leq2d^{b'}$ and $1+\ln(8Dk)\leq4(b_n+1)(1+\ln d)$. Since
$B(b_n+1)\leq3n+1$, the source purity obeys
\[
 \log_2(m/k)\leq4\log_2n+2\log_2\bigl(B(b_n+1)\bigr)+2\log_2(1+\ln d)+O(1)
 \leq s_n,
\]
where $s_n=6\log_2n+2\log_2(1+\ln d)+c$ and $c$ is an absolute
constant. Also $\log_2m\leq\frac{b_n}2\log_2r+1+s_n$ for $b'=b_n$.

The memory holds a payload bank of $b_n\lceil\log_2d\rceil$ qubits, a
bath bank of $\log_2m$ qubits sized for $b'=b_n$, and a pure reversible
clock of $\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$ qubits. The inputs of a batch enter
the payload bank as they arrive. After the last one, the collective
unitary acts on the payload and bath banks in place, and the outputs are
released. Between batches the bath bank is exported, and a fresh one is
imported in the lemma's source state: $\log_2k$ maximally mixed qubits
and $\log_2(m/k)$ pure ones. A shorter final batch uses its own smaller
$k$ and $m$. Its unused bath qubits are imported maximally mixed and are
never touched, so they cost no purity.

No output is released inside a batch. So the inputs of a batch form one
joint input, entangled with the observer's reference and with all
earlier outputs. The batch applies a channel within half-diamond
distance $\delta$ of $\Psi^{\otimes b'}$. Its bath is then exported for
good, the next batch starts from a fresh independent import, and the
clock is pure and never meets an input. Replace the batch channels by
$\Psi^{\otimes b'}$ one at a time. At each step the state entering the
batch is unchanged and everything after it is a fixed channel, so the
observer's final state moves by at most $\delta$. The complete adaptive
error is at most $B\delta=n^{-2}$.

The first bath and the clock give $P\leq s_n+\log_2n+2$, and the $B-1$
imports give $C\leq(B-1)s_n$. With $B-1\leq n/b_n$,
\begin{align*}
 P+C&\leq Bs_n+\log_2n+2,\qquad
 J=(B-1)\log_2m\leq\tfrac n2\log_2r+\frac{(1+s_n)n}{b_n},\\
 Q_{\rm peak}&\leq\bigl(\lceil\log_2d\rceil+\tfrac12\log_2r\bigr)b_n
 +s_n+\log_2n+3.
\end{align*}
Hence $(P+C)/n\leq6z_n+(s_n-6\log_2n)/b_n+(s_n+\log_2n+2)/n$.

\emph{Choice.} For each $n$, use collective batches if $6z_n<\log_2r$,
and exact banks otherwise. In the first case $b_n>6\log_2n/\log_2r$.
The two error terms are then $O_d(1/\log n)$,
$J\leq\frac32n\log_2r+o(n)$, and $Q_{\rm peak}=O(b_n+\log n)=o(n)$. In
both cases the contract holds and
\[
 \frac{P+C}n\leq\min\{\log_2r,6z_n\}+o(1).
\]
The map $t\mapsto\min\{\log_2r,6t\}$ is continuous and nondecreasing on
$[0,\infty]$, so the limsup of the right side is $\min\{\log_2r,6x\}$.
This proves the bound, including $x=\infty$.

If $r=1$, exact banks have $J=0$. If $r\geq2$ and $b_n/\log n\to\infty$,
then $z_n\to0$ and collective batches are used for all large $n$. Since $\log_2m\geq\log_2k\geq\frac{b_n}2\log_2r$ and
$B-1\geq n/b_n-1$, also $J\geq\frac n2\log_2r-\frac{b_n}2\log_2r$.
Hence $J=\frac n2\log_2r+o(n)$.
\end{proof}
