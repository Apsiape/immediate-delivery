\section{The exact entropic profile and the quantitative memory law}\label{sec:entropic}

An exact support certificate specifies what a round does on its source, without requiring a flat source or
global multiplication of rectangular responses. That is enough for a chronological compiler. It is also the
right static object to extract from high-precision services.

\begin{definition}[Exact-support entropy profile]\label{def:exact-profile}
For a canonical untwisted anchored gadget, take finite spaces $K,L$, a state $\rho$ on $K$, and isometries
$B_g:K\to L$ satisfying
\[
 B_1^*B_{[y]}=B_{[x]}^*B_{[z]},\qquad
 \max_{g\ne h}|\Tr(\rho B_g^*B_h)|\le1/2,
\]
the first identity for every triangle $(x,y,z)$ of the table, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:dilation}(a).
Put $\sigma=\sum_g\mu_g B_g\rho B_g^*$ and $a=S(\sigma)-S(\rho)$. Define, for $t\ge0$,
\[
 Z_\Phi(t)=\inf\{S(\rho)+t a\}
\]
over these families. One may restrict $K$ to $\supp\rho$ and $L$ to $\spn_g B_gK$, so $\dim L\le r\dim K$.
\end{definition}

The family extends to a physical unitary round, and $a$ is its actual Choi-input production.
It is nonnegative because the isometric output ensemble has the source spectrum. Exact support does not mean
exact target error: the coarse trace gap is amplified when designing a service.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-matrix form and profile structure]\label{prop:Z-matrix}
For source dimension $k$, an equivalent optimization uses a positive block matrix $Y=[Y_{gh}]$ and a state $\rho$,
with $Y_{gg}=I$, $Y_{1[y]}=Y_{[x][z]}$ for every triangle $(x,y,z)$, and $|\Tr\rho Y_{gh}|\le1/2$ for $g\ne h$.
Writing $D=\diag(\sqrt{\mu_g})$, the state
\[
 \omega=(D\otimes\sqrt\rho)Y(D\otimes\sqrt\rho)
\]
has source marginal $\rho$ and $a=S(\omega)-S(\rho)$. The function $Z_\Phi$ is nondecreasing, concave and subadditive,
$Z_\Phi(0)=0$, $Z_\Phi(t)\le t\log r$, and $Z_\Phi(ct)\le cZ_\Phi(t)$ for $c\ge1$.
Moreover, for $t_r=\lceil\log_2(8(r-1))\rceil$,
\[
 L^*_\Phi(n)\le t_r Z_\Phi(n).
\]
\end{proposition}

The spectrum identity follows from the two products of the row operator
$[\sqrt{\mu_g}B_g\sqrt\rho]_g$. Nonnegativity is a property of this ensemble; quantum conditional entropy is
not positive in general. Infima of the affine costs $S+t a$ give concavity. Fixed tensor amplification preserves
the untwisted identities and powers the scalar trace overlaps. Its output is the \emph{mixture of powered outputs},
not the tensor power of their mixture. Appendix~\ref{app:static} proves these assertions.

\begin{theorem}[Coherent exact-support compiler]\label{thm:coherent}
Every family in Definition~\ref{def:exact-profile} gives, for $n\ge1$ and $0<\epsilon\le1$, a fixed public service
of the canonical target with complete adaptive error at most $\epsilon$ and
\[
 Q\le C_{d,r}\log_2^2\!\left(\frac{128(n+1)}\epsilon\right)(1+S+n a),\quad
 J\le\lfloor(n-1)\log r/2\rfloor,\quad C=0,\quad P\le Q.
\]
The constant is independent of source dimension, spectrum and response geometry.
\end{theorem}

The construction replaces the biased source by maximally mixed registers. Each eigenvalue of the source, and
of the output state, is coded as a block whose dimension is proportional to it, inside sectors of geometrically
increasing dimension; this is the standard embedding of nonuniform states into flat ones from thermodynamic
resource theory~\cite{HorodeckiOppenheim2013}. The sector index then acts as an energy, and one use changes it by the logarithm of the
ratio of an output eigenvalue to a source eigenvalue. Tensor amplification bounds the moment generating function
of these changes as a matrix over pairs of labels, which covers coherent sources. Excess dimension is exported and
replaced by maximally mixed qubits. One induction over the rounds bounds both the chance of leaving the allowed
range of sectors and the error of the whole adaptive experiment. The device never measures the source or
postselects. Appendix~\ref{app:graded} gives the construction and its resource count.

\begin{theorem}[Any-exchange necessity and high precision]\label{thm:Z-necessary}
Every between-round $n$-round service of the canonical gadget, at any exchange, with error $0<\epsilon\le1/16$ satisfies
\[
 Z_\Phi(n)\le K_{d,r}\left[1+Q+C+\sqrt{n\epsilon}\log_2(en/\epsilon)\right].
\]
If $\epsilon\le1/[n\log_2^2(en)]$, this reduces to $1+Z_\Phi(n)\le K'_{d,r}(1+Q+C)$.
There is no exchange restriction. With flat imports, Theorem~\ref{thm:coherent} gives the matching rank-rate
upper up to the displayed squared logarithm.
\end{theorem}

Extraction chooses a round with leakage $O(\epsilon/n)$ and production paid by $(P+C)/n$.
Biased first-order repair makes support exact at additional cost
$O(\sqrt{n\epsilon}\log(en/\epsilon))$.
Keeping the experiment error is essential: setting it to $1/16$ first loses the high-precision conclusion.
Its proof is independent of the compiler (Appendix~\ref{app:static}).

\begin{theorem}[Memory from one-use entropy]\label{thm:main}
Fix a canonical untwisted anchored gadget on $M_d$ with Choi rank
$r\geq2$. Write $y=\log_2(128(n+1)/\epsilon)$, where
$0<\epsilon\leq1/16$. Constants depend only on $d,r$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=2pt]
\item Every service with exchange $J\leq n\log_2r/2$ has
$Q\geq c_{d,r}L^*_\Phi(n)-C_{d,r}\log_2(en)$.
\item A service exists with maximally mixed imports, initial purity
$P\leq Q$, exchange $J\leq\lfloor(n-1)\log_2r/2\rfloor$ and
$Q\leq C_{d,r}y^2(1+Z_\Phi(n))$.
\item At precision $\epsilon\leq1/[n\log_2^2(en)]$,
\[
 c_{d,r}(1+Z_\Phi(n))\leq1+\Qf(n,\epsilon)
       \leq C_{d,r}y^2(1+Z_\Phi(n)),
\]
where $\Qf$ minimizes memory under the flat-import contract in (b).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The converse uses Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}; the construction and high-precision
comparison use Theorems~\ref{thm:coherent} and~\ref{thm:Z-necessary}.
At fixed error the explicit converse correction grows with $n$, which
is why part (c) states its precision hypothesis.

\begin{theorem}[Horizon envelope]\label{thm:envelope}
For a fixed threshold $N_0(d,r)$, put
\[
 E_\Phi(n)=\inf_{N\ge\max(n,N_0)}
 \left[L^*_\Phi(N)+\frac n{\sqrt N}\log_2(eN)\right],
\]
where $N$ is integer. Then $Z_\Phi(n)\le C_{d,r}(1+E_\Phi(n))$ and
\[
 \Qf(n,\epsilon)\le C_{d,r}\min\left\{n,\ y^2(1+E_\Phi(n))\right\},
 \qquad y=\log_2(128(n+1)/\epsilon).
\]
No regularity of $L^*_\Phi$ is assumed.
\end{theorem}

Choose a round at the design horizon $N$, repair its leakage at that scale, and serve $n\le N$ rounds.
At polynomial inverse error, $L^*_\Phi(N)=N^{\beta+o(1)}$ gives the generic upper exponent
$\beta/(\beta+1/2)$ for $0<\beta<1/2$ and $\beta$ for $\beta\ge1/2$.
The lower exponent is $\beta$. Polylogarithmic profiles give polylogarithmic services and subpolynomial
profiles give subpolynomial services, but the envelope does not close the interval below $1/2$.

\paragraph{The missing theorem.}
A universal all-accuracy rank-rate upper bound on $\Qf$ through $L^*_\Phi(n)$, with flat imports, is equivalent, up to finite
logarithmic powers, to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:missing-comparison}
 Z_\Phi(n)\le K_\Phi\log_2^p(en)(1+L^*_\Phi(n))
\end{equation}
for some finite $p$. Sufficiency is Theorem~\ref{thm:coherent}; necessity applies
Theorem~\ref{thm:Z-necessary} at $\epsilon_n=1/[16n\log_2^2(en)]$.
This is a comparison of optimized static costs. It permits choosing an exact family wholly different from
the original leaky round. Requiring a close repair of every round is stronger and can fail.

\paragraph{Other descriptions of the same cost.}
Appendix~\ref{app:static} gives two equivalent descriptions of $Z_\Phi$. First, a family whose
source-weighted trace defect is at most $c_r/n$ can be repaired to an exact one, at a cost bounded by a constant multiple of its
own cost plus $\log(en)$ (equation~\eqref{eq:affine-profile}), without filtering the source. Second, an optimization over ensembles of square unitaries,
penalized by entropy, matches $Z_\Phi$ within constants. Neither proves~\eqref{eq:missing-comparison}:
the ensemble's entropy gain need not be that of a physical round, and ordinary leakage controls the trace
defect only at the square-root scale.

\paragraph{A sufficient probability certificate.}
There is one useful setting in which support probability can be used
directly. Let $\Lambda$ be the positive bath leakage operator, normalized
so that $\ell=\Tr(\rho\Lambda)/d$. If the round has distance $u\le1/16$
and the source has mass $1-O(1/n)$ on $\ker\Lambda$, then for all
sufficiently large $n$, even without commutation between the source and
that kernel projection,
\[
 Z_\Phi(n)\leq C_{d,r}\log_2(en)(1+S+na).
\]
Appendix~\ref{app:static} proves the dimension-free source projection
estimate and its coherent permutation corollary. Small mean leakage
alone does not imply this hypothesis: the quartic example there has
an empty exact kernel and nevertheless a cheap, different exact finite tracial factorization.
