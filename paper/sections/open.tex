\section{What remains open}\label{sec:open}

The high-precision law leaves a quantitative fixed-error question.
The following four problems separate it from the sharper Houghton
rate, entropy-rate service and autonomous local realization.

\begin{problem}[The concentration conjecture]\label{prob:keep-bias}
For every canonical untwisted anchored gadget $\Phi$, do constants
$C_\Phi<\infty$ and $p<\infty$ exist such that, for all $n\geq1$,
\[
 Z_\Phi(n)\leq C_\Phi\log_2^p(en)(1+L^*_\Phi(n))?
\]
\end{problem}

A positive answer would complete the universal memory comparison at
all accuracies (Section~\ref{sec:entropic}).

\paragraph{Matrix form.}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:Z-matrix}, $Z_\Phi(n)$ minimizes
$S(\rho)+na$ over exact solutions: a state $\rho$ and a positive block
matrix $Y=[Y_{gh}]$ with $Y_{gg}=I$, $Y_{1[y]}=Y_{[x][z]}$ for every
triangle $(x,y,z)$, and $|\Tr\rho Y_{gh}|\leq1/2$ for $g\ne h$.
The profile $L^*_\Phi(n)$ minimizes the same cost over near-solutions:
rounds that may leave the Kraus span, with probability at most
$2/(15n)$ when the bath is in state $\rho$ and the input is maximally
mixed. The defect is thus weighted by the source $\rho$, not by the
normalized trace. The conjecture asks whether every cheap
$\rho$-weighted near-solution can be traded for an exact solution, of
any size and with any source, at a polylogarithmic increase in cost.
The exact solution need not be close to the near-solution.

\paragraph{Relation to group stability.}
Stability asks whether almost-homomorphisms of a group are close to
homomorphisms. In the Frobenius norm, cohomology vanishing gives
stability and yields finitely presented groups that are not
approximated by unitary matrices~\cite{DeChiffreEtAl2020}. In the
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt metric, infinite hyperlinear groups with
property~(T) are not stable, and Becker and Lubotzky proposed a
flexible notion that allows the space to be enlarged before
correcting~\cite[Section~4.4]{BeckerLubotzky2020}. The conjecture is
flexible in the same way. It differs in two respects: the defect is
weighted by the source state $\rho$ rather than by the normalized
trace, and the price is the entropy cost $S+na$ rather than a distance
or a dimension. No reduction between the two questions is known.

\paragraph{Where the conjecture is open.}
The conjecture holds in regimes (i) and (iii) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy}. In regime (i), an exact finite tracial
factorization is an exact solution with zero production, so $Z_\Phi$ is
bounded. In regime (iii), $L^*_\Phi(n)$ is linear, while
$Z_\Phi(n)\leq n\log r$. In regime (ii) the proved repair gives
$Z(n)\leq C[1+L^*(n)+\sqrt n\log n]$, together with the sharper
horizon envelope, so matching holds above the square-root scale up to
logarithms. The open range is regime (ii) below $\sqrt n$.
Concentration on an exact kernel and weighted affine trace defect
$O(1/n)$ are sufficient mechanisms; mean support leakage $O(1/n)$
alone implies neither. Exact rectangular frames can satisfy the local
gadget identities without extending to global group representations,
so representation repair addresses a stronger question.

The first concrete target is Houghton's channel $\Phi_H$. It lies in
regime (ii), and $L^*_{\Phi_H}(n)\leq Cn^{1/3}$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling-houghton}), below the scale where the
proved repair gives matching bounds.

\paragraph{A permutation subcase.}
For permutation rounds, triangle-bad points have mass
$O(1/n)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:perm-leak}. One may ask for an exact
replacement whose entropy and production remain within
logarithmic factors of the original cost. Collisions and the
entropy cost of the good-point restriction still need control.
Houghton's $H_3$ is not P-stable: it is amenable, hence sofic,
and not residually finite, whereas a sofic P-stable group must
be residually finite~\cite{GlebskyRivera2009},
\cite[Theorem~7.2(ii)]{ArzhantsevaPaunescu2015},
\cite[discussion after Proposition~8.5]{BeckerLubotzkyThom2019}.
So no general nearby repair by permutation representations exists for
$H_3$. This leaves open whether the optimized costs separate, and
whether unrestricted quantum devices outperform permutation ones.

\paragraph{The flat subcase.}
A useful special case asks whether every coarse-correct flat round with
leakage $O(1/n)$ admits an exact family of cost
$\mathrm{polylog}(n)(1+S+na)$, allowing a larger bath and fixed
coarse-error slack. Rounds with exactly zero production, of any source
spectrum, reduce to a finite tracial factorization at entropy-scale
dimension with a multiplicative leakage loss (Appendix~\ref{app:static}).
Positive production remains outside that reduction. The second-order
obstruction of Appendix~\ref{sec:flat} concerns twisted identities,
while this conjecture concerns untwisted optimized costs.

\begin{problem}[All-family Houghton sharpness]\label{prob:houghton}
Is $L^*_{\Phi_H}(n)\geq n^{1/3}/\mathrm{polylog}(n)$? More weakly,
is $Q^*_{\Phi_H}(n,\epsilon)\geq n^{1/3-o(1)}$ at fixed
$\epsilon\leq1/16$?
\end{problem}

A positive answer would show that the cube-root constructions remain
optimal beyond genuine group devices. Table~\ref{tab:houghton-scope} in Appendix~\ref{app:houghton-sources}
separates the operational theorem from the static bounds for explicit
smoothed rounds. Those bounds reach the cube-root scale for a moderate
number of tensor copies and for stationary sources. For general sources
with positive production they lie between the quarter- and cube-root
scales. Small production has not been shown to force a source near the
stationary algebra, and a uniform spectral estimate for the killed
Laplacian of Appendix~\ref{app:houghton-coercivity} is open. Other
rounds, and mixtures over clock sizes with positive production, remain
beyond these bounds. The one-round operational method has its stated
$n^{1/6}$ ceiling; a lower bound for $Z$ would need a separate
all-family argument.

\begin{problem}[Low-purity and nonflat-probe entropy-rate service]\label{prob:closure-purity}
For $\Phi\in\overline{\mathcal F}$ and
$\log(1/\epsilon_n)=o(n)$, can every flat-probe target be served
with maximally mixed imports, $J\leq\lfloor(n-1)\log r/2\rfloor$,
$Q=o(n)$ and $P=O(\log(n/\epsilon_n))$?
For arbitrary nonflat probes, does sublinear service with maximally
mixed imports hold at exchange rate $H(\Phi)/2$, where $H(\Phi)$
is the maximum entropy of a complementary output?
\end{problem}

These questions test whether sublinear memory can coexist with small
initial purity, and whether rank can be replaced by complementary
entropy in the general compiler. The principal upper constructions charge $P\leq Q$;
the nonflat rank-rate compiler does not give a half-complementary-entropy
import threshold. With unrestricted imported purity the any-exchange
converse controls $Q+C$: a pure replacer can recycle one retained
qubit using one fresh pure qubit after each round.

\paragraph{Spatial and spectral refinements.}
For devices that move points along a Schreier graph through nested sets
with balanced boundaries (the collar devices of Section~\ref{sec:biased}),
and for the configuration-lamp devices, the sharp memory rates are
open, independently of the general compiler. The rank-based profile
$U_\Phi$ and the entropy-based profiles may also differ: source entropy
charges the occupied spectrum, while $U_\Phi$ charges the logarithm of
the ambient rank. How far apart they can be is open.

By an \emph{exact stationary compression} we mean the following.
Start a finite-dimensional probe coupled to a spin reservoir in
its tracial product state, evolve by a fixed finite-range
interaction, and trace out the reservoir. The reduced channels
$\Psi_t$ converge as $t\to\infty$ to $\Phi$ on every probe input,
including a reference (equivalently, in diamond norm at fixed
probe dimension). The target is this exact limit, rather than
one selected finite-time approximation. All controllers and
their initial states must belong to the specified dynamics.

\begin{problem}[An expensive autonomous local stationary limit]\label{prob:local-limit}
Is a channel with a super-polylogarithmic finite-mixture profile the
exact stationary compression of finite-range quantum spin dynamics
in a tracial product reservoir?
\end{problem}

Such a construction would connect finite-memory approximation cost to
an autonomous local reservoir. Known local-dynamics arguments exclude
only narrow classes, so autonomous local realization remains open.
This construction question is independent of the concentration conjecture.

\paragraph{Repeatability without resets.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:repeatable}, with the catalytic dilation of Lie,
Son, Boes, Ng and Wilming, answers Ryb\'ar and Ziman's
question~\cite{RybarZiman2008}: some unital channels that are not
random-unitary are repeatable, and some unital channels are not. What
remains is to identify the repeatable channels. Catalytic channels are
repeatable, and repeatable channels are finite mixtures of finite
tracial factorizations. Lie, Son, Boes, Ng and Wilming show that the
catalytic channels form a strict subset of these
mixtures~\cite[Appendix~C]{LieSonBoesNgWilming2026}, so at least one of
the two inclusions is strict. We do not know which. A repeatable device
must reproduce the channel at every use, but its memory state may move;
a catalytic device keeps it fixed. Haagerup, Musat and Ruskai's channels $\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}$ on $M_3$ are
extreme among unital channels for $|\alpha|^2\notin\{0,\frac12,1\}$, are
not unitary, and have finite tracial factorizations with one bath
qubit~\cite{HaagerupMusatRuskai2021}. They are not
catalytic~\cite[Appendix~C]{LieSonBoesNgWilming2026}. If one of them is
repeatable, the catalytic channels are a strict subset of the repeatable
ones. If one of them is not, the repeatable channels are a strict subset
of the finite mixtures.
