\section{A bounded mixture profile for phased monomial dynamics}\label{sec:monomial}
%======================================================================

This appendix isolates a tractable structured case of the mixture
profile used in Section~\ref{sec:closure}. For phased-monomial
dynamics, a finite tile description bounds each component bath
uniformly in the approximation accuracy. The argument concerns
mixtures, not a bounded exact single-factor realization.

Fix product bases on $\C^d\otimes\C^M$. A phased-monomial unitary has exactly one nonzero entry of modulus one in each row and column.
Let $\mathcal C_{\rm mon}(d)$ be its reduced channels with bath $I_M/M$, for arbitrary finite $M$, and let
$\mathcal F_B^{\rm mon}$ be finite convex mixtures of such factors with component bath dimension at most $B$.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded exact mixture]\label{thm:monomial-mixture}
Put
\[
 E(d)=\sum_{k=1}^d\binom dk^2k!,\qquad
 B(d)=d\left\lceil(d+1)^{(E(d)+1)/2}\right\rceil .
\]
Then $\mathcal C_{\rm mon}(d)\subseteq\mathcal F_{B(d)}^{\rm mon}$, this latter set is compact, and
$\overline{\mathcal C_{\rm mon}(d)}=\mathcal F_{B(d)}^{\rm mon}$.
Every member is a mixture of at most $d^4+1$ components. In particular, for every $\Phi\in\overline{\mathcal C_{\rm mon}(d)}$,
\[
 D_{J,\Phi}(\gamma)\le\left\lceil(d^4+1)B(d)/\gamma\right\rceil,\qquad 0<\gamma\le1.
\]
No bounded exact single-factor bath is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An edge label $e$ is a nonempty partial bijection $A_e\to B_e$ of subsets of $[d]$; there are $E(d)$ labels.
An input tile is a collection of labels whose domains partition $[d]$, an output tile one whose ranges partition $[d]$.
Each contains at most $d$ edges; repeated labels inside a tile are impossible.
For a monomial unitary, group the system slots of an input bath point $b$ by their output bath point $y$.
Each group gives one edge, and the resulting input tile is $T_b$. Reversing the grouping gives the output tiles $S_y$.

Let $x_T,y_S$ be normalized tile counts. They lie in the rational polytope
\[
 x,y\ge0,\quad\sum_Tx_T=1,\quad
 \sum_{T:e\in T}x_T=\sum_{S:e\in S}y_S\quad(e\text{ any edge}).
\]
Multiplying these balances by $|A_e|$ and summing gives $\sum_Sy_S=1$, so the polytope is compact.
The constraint matrix has $E(d)+1$ rows, entries $0,\pm1$, and each column has Euclidean norm at most $\sqrt{d+1}$.
A vertex has at most $E(d)+1$ positive coordinates with independent columns.
Selecting a nonsingular square minor and using Cramer's rule and Hadamard's determinant bound gives a common denominator
\[
 D\le\left\lceil(d+1)^{(E(d)+1)/2}\right\rceil .
\]
Realize $Dx_T$ input points and $Dy_S$ output points. Pair equally labelled edge stubs using the balances.
This gives a bipartite multigraph, but parallel edges between a pair of bath points must not be left as separate Kraus terms:
they would create coherences absent from the tile formula.

Take a $d$-fold cyclic cover. For every parallel-edge family assign distinct shifts in $\Z_d$; there are at most $d$ edges in a family.
An edge with shift $a$ joins $(b,t)$ to $(y,t+a)$.
Thus no covered bath pair carries two edges. The domain partition at each input and range partition at each output make the covered
system--bath map a genuine permutation unitary, with bath dimension $dD\le B(d)$.

For a tile $T$ and phases $|z_s|=1$, define
\[
 V_{e,z}=\sum_{s\in A_e}z_s\ket{e(s)}\bra s,\qquad
 \mathcal E_{T,z}(X)=\sum_{e\in T}V_{e,z}XV_{e,z}^* .
\]
The original channel is $\sum_Tx_T\overline{\mathcal E}_T$, where the bar is the conditional phase average over input bath points with tile $T$.
This identity includes all coherences. Decompose $(x,y)$ into vertices of the tile polytope.
For each vertex realization, sample independently at its covered input points the original conditional phase law of that point's tile.
Because the cover separates bath pairs, its expected channel is exactly the corresponding vertex tile average.
Averaging vertices recovers the original channel.

For bath sizes up to $B(d)$ there are finitely many permutations and compact phase tori; their channels form a compact set.
Its convex hull is compact in the finite-dimensional real channel space, and Carath\'eodory reduces the averages to at most $d^4+1$
actual components. This proves containment and compactness.
Conversely every finite mixture of these components is approximated by block copies of finite tracial factorizations with identity filler
(Lemma~\ref{lem:mixture-flat}), which are again phased monomial. This proves the closure equality and single-factor profile bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Product-diagonal-preserving finite-radius dynamics]\label{cor:monomial-qca}
Consider a reversible finite-radius quantum cellular automaton on a finite-dimensional lattice with product trace.
If it preserves the product diagonal maximal abelian algebra, every finite-time reduced channel of a fixed $d$-dimensional probe,
and every ordinary or Ces\`aro limit of these channels, belongs to $\mathcal F_{B(d)}^{\rm mon}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At any finite time the probe matrix algebra embeds into a finite window $M_d\otimes M_M$.
The images $w_{ab}$ of its matrix units are \emph{partial} normalizers of the window diagonal algebra, not generally unitaries.
Since $w_{aa}$ is diagonal and $w_{ab}$ is a partial isometry normalizing diagonals, it has at most one nonzero entry per row and column,
each of modulus one. Trace preservation gives $\rank w_{aa}=M$.
Choose a basis $\{v_b\}_{b=1}^M$ of $\operatorname{ran}w_{11}$ from the preferred window basis.
The vectors $w_{a1}v_b$ form that full basis up to phases, so
$W\ket{a,b}=w_{a1}v_b$ is a phased-monomial unitary implementing the embedding.
Theorem~\ref{thm:monomial-mixture} applies, and compactness passes to limits and time averages.
\end{proof}

This statement concerns maximally mixed baths and a specified product diagonal algebra. It does not prohibit a hard channel from Gaussian baths
with arbitrary non-Gaussian controllers, from blocked or dressed diagonal structures, or from general quantum local dynamics.
Physically incoherent operations have a related projector--phase-permutation description~\cite{ChitambarGour2016}; the theorem's bath
cap and exact-mixture assertion are stated without a priority claim.
