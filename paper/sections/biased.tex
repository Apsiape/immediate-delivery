\section{Conserved bias and spatial constructions}\label{sec:biased}
%======================================================================

A round whose bath is not flat is \emph{biased}. Flattening a biased bath is not free: gluing the level sets of the bath into a flat bath
pays the square root of the entropy production, because level sets are $\ell^1$ objects while entropy production is an $\ell^2$ quantity
(Theorem~\ref{thm:FK}). Nor can a device simply re-randomize a biased memory, since that flattens it and fills the rank-grown space
(Theorem~\ref{thm:global-haar}). Theorem~\ref{thm:coherent} supplies general exact-support streaming. This appendix gives specialized
conserved-bias and spatial constructions, followed by the biased leakage repair used in the main profile bounds.
Throughout, $(B_g)$ denotes the gadget family of a zero-leakage round on $K=\supp\rho$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:zero-leak}), and
$G_{gh}=\Tr(\rho B_g^*B_h)$ its weighted Gram.

\subsection{Tensor powers and conserved bias}

\begin{lemma}[Tensor powers]\label{lem:tensor}
For a zero-leakage round $(U,\rho)$ of a gadget channel and $t\ge1$, the family $(B_g^{\otimes t})$ extends to a round with bath $\rho^{\otimes t}$,
leakage $0$, weighted Gram $(G_{gh}^t)$, distance at most $\frac12(r-1)\max_{g\ne h}|G_{gh}|^t$, bath entropy $tS$ and entropy production at most
$t\,a$. In particular, if $|G_{gh}|\le c<1$ for $g\ne h$ and $t=\lceil\log(4rn/\epsilon)/\log(1/c)\rceil$, every off-diagonal is at most $\epsilon/(4rn)$.
\end{lemma}

So a weighted Gram bounded away from one costs only a factor $O(\log(n/\epsilon))$, for any bath. The first class that streams is the one in
which the bias is a conserved quantity of the round.

\begin{theorem}[Conserved bias]\label{thm:conserved}
Let $(U,\rho)$ be a zero-leakage round of a canonical gadget with a
finitely supported level law $(w_s)$, such that $K=\bigoplus_sE_s$, $e_s=\dim E_s$, $\rho=\bigoplus_s(w_s/e_s)I_{E_s}$, there are
mutually orthogonal subspaces $E'_s$ with $B_gE_s\subseteq E'_s$ for all $g$ and $s$, and the weighted Gram has off-diagonals at most $\frac18$
(distance $u\le\frac1{16}$ suffices). The per-level Grams may be arbitrary. Then $a=\sum_sw_sa_s$ exactly, with $a_s\ge0$ the entropy
production of level $s$ at flat input, and for $n\ge2$ and $0<\epsilon<1$ there is a rank-rate service with error at most $2\epsilon$, $C=0$ and
$Q\le K_{d,r}\log^3(n/\epsilon)(1+S+n\,a)$, with $t=\lceil\log(4rn/\epsilon)/2\rceil$. When $1.07\,ta\le1$ its memory also satisfies
\[
Q\le t(32S+5.1)+C_{d,r}\,n\,(1.07\,ta)\log\frac e{1.07\,ta}+O_{d,r}\bigl(\log^2(n/\epsilon)+\log(2+tS)\bigr).
\] Without any device: such a round gives a zero-leakage flat round with flat Gram off-diagonals at most
$\frac14$, $S'\le32S+5.1$ and $a'\le1.07\,a$.
\end{theorem}

The device truncates the levels at surprisal $32S$, which costs no binary entropy because $a$ is a conserved average, flattens the level index
into a flat bath with multiplicities proportional to $w_s/e_s$, and streams by Theorem~\ref{thm:flat-zero}(c). So conserved bias never beats
flat rounds by more than constants. Leaky rounds of this kind stream too, up to the additive term of Corollary~\ref{cor:F}; the tool is a
restriction that commutes with the bath.

\begin{lemma}[Restriction for biased baths]\label{lem:restrict-biased}
Let $(U,\rho)$ be any round and $P_0$ a projection commuting with $\rho$, with $\nu=\Tr((I-P_0)\rho)<1$ and $\rho=(1-\nu)\rho_0\oplus\nu\rho_\perp$.
Then $S(\rho_0)\le S(\rho)/(1-\nu)$, the round $(U,\rho_0)$ has distance at most $(u+\nu)/(1-\nu)$, leakage at most $\ell/(1-\nu)$ and
\[
a(\rho_0)\le\frac{a+h_2(\nu)}{1-\nu},
\]
and its leakage is $0$ if $U$ has the form $\sum_iA_i\otimes X_i$ on $\C^d\otimes\operatorname{ran}P_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[Leaky conserved bias]\label{cor:conserved-leaky}
Let $(U,\rho)$ be a round of a canonical gadget with a finitely
supported level law $(w_s)$ and $K=\bigoplus_sE_s$, $\rho=\bigoplus_s(w_s/e_s)I_{E_s}$, and, with $W=U(I\otimes\iota_K)$ for the inclusion
$\iota_K$ of $K$, $W(\C^d\otimes E_s)\subseteq\C^d\otimes E'_s$ with the
$E'_s$ mutually orthogonal, distance $u\le\frac1{16}$ and leakage $\ell\le c_1/n$. For $n\ge n_{CB}(d,r,c_1)$ and $0<\epsilon<1$ there is a rank-rate service
with error at most $2\epsilon$, $C=0$ and $Q\le K'\log^4(n/\epsilon)(1+S+n\,a+\sqrt n)$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Rounds that are vacuous for the law}

A zero-leakage round of a channel whose labels a finite-dimensional representation separates needs no device: the channel has logarithmic
memory (Corollary~\ref{cor:map}). The following lemma shows that a zero-leakage round can only be ``finite-dimensional'' in that sense.

\begin{lemma}[Compressions of finite-dimensional representations]\label{lem:vacuous}
Let $(U,\rho)$ be a zero-leakage round of a gadget channel with distance $u<\frac12$. If $B_g^*B_h=J^*\pi(g)^*\pi(h)J$ for a unitary representation
$\pi$ of $P_\Phi$ on a finite-dimensional space and an isometry $J$, then $\pi$ separates the labels, and $\Qs(n,\epsilon)=O_\Phi(\log(n/\epsilon))$. This
applies to every \emph{square} round ($B_gK\subseteq K$ for every label), abelian or not.
\end{lemma}

So for a channel that needs more than logarithmic memory, every zero-leakage round at distance below $\frac12$ is non-square, and its Gram
operators are not jointly a compression of a finite-dimensional representation.

\subsection{Permutation rounds and the balance condition}

A family is \emph{block-monomial} if $K=\bigoplus_{\omega\in\Omega_0}K_\omega$, there are mutually orthogonal subspaces $K'_\eta$ ($\eta\in\Omega\supseteq\Omega_0$)
with $K'_\omega=K_\omega$ for $\omega\in\Omega_0$, and each $B_g$ maps $K_\omega$ isometrically onto $K'_{f(g)\omega}$ for maps $f(g)$ with $f(1)=\id$; it is
\emph{free} if $f(g)\omega\ne f(h)\omega$ for $g\ne h$ and $\omega\in\Omega_0$. A bath is block diagonal if $\rho=\bigoplus_\omega\rho_\omega$, with base law
$p(\omega)=\Tr\rho_\omega$.

\begin{lemma}[Free block-monomial rounds]\label{lem:block-monomial}
In a free block-monomial zero-leakage round with a block-diagonal bath, the base family $\lambda_g\ket\omega=\ket{f(g)\omega}$ is a gadget family, and
with the bath $p$ it defines an exact round (Lemma~\ref{lem:dilation}(c)) with $S_{\rm base}=H(p)\le S(\rho)$ and $a_{\rm base}\le a$. Here each $f(g)$ is an
injection $\Omega_0\to\Omega$; the base round is a free exact permutation round when these injections extend to permutations of $\Omega$
that are triangle-good on the support of $p$.
\end{lemma}

So it suffices to stream free exact permutation rounds. The devices for them keep a set of points, twirl a few slots attached to each
point, and move the points around inside fixed windows.

\begin{theorem}[Point-block devices]\label{thm:pointblock}
Let a free permutation round of a gadget channel be given on $\Omega$, with windows $\Omega_0\subseteq\dots\subseteq\Omega_n\subseteq\Omega$ fixed in advance
on which it is triangle-good and collision-free. Consider the device whose memory is $\C^{\Omega}\otimes\C^m$ with point blocks
$\ket\omega\otimes\C^m$, which starts in $p_0\otimes I/m$ for a law $p_0$ on $\Omega_0$ and in round $t$ applies a fixed permutation $\beta_t$ of the points of
$\Omega_{t-1}$, independent Haar unitaries on the point blocks, the round, an optional fixed partial injection of points, sends the points outside
$\Omega_t$ to a garbage register by a fixed unitary completion, applies Haar unitaries on the new point blocks and exports on the schedule
of~\cite[Theorem~12.2]{DouglasMghirbiC}. Let $\kappa(y)$ be the probability that the \emph{ideal killed chain} of the label sequence $y$ (start from
$p_0$, permute by $\beta_t$, move by $f(y_t)$ and the injection, kill outside $\Omega_t$) is killed, and $\omega_{\rm ideal}=\sum_y\Pr(y)\kappa(y)$ for the
observer's ideal law of label sequences. For $n\ge2$, $0<\epsilon<1$ and $0<\zeta\le\epsilon^2/(64n)$ there is $m$ with
$\log m=O_r(\log(n/\zeta)+\log\log|\Omega|)$ such that for every observer the discarded weight is at most $\omega_{\rm ideal}+\zeta$, the error is at most
$(1+\sqrt n)\sqrt{\omega_{\rm ideal}+\epsilon^2/(16n)}$, and at the start of every round the memory has mass at most $n(\omega_{\rm ideal}+\zeta)$ outside the
window. The memory is $\log\max_t|\Omega_t|+\log m+O(1)$ qubits at the rank rate.
\end{theorem}

The quantum side of the device is thus unconditional; what remains is the containment of a classical chain. Discarded branches can come back to
the window through the unitary completion, which is why the error is $(1+\sqrt n)$ times the square root of the discarded weight and not the
weight itself. Random permutations inside classes of points make the chain mix.

\begin{lemma}[Random class permutations]\label{lem:classperm}
Fix windows and partitions of each $\Omega_{t-1}$ into \emph{classes} in advance, and let $\beta_t$ be independent uniformly random permutations of
each class; a fixed partial injection of points after each round is allowed. Let $\kappa_{\rm class}(y)$ be the kill probability of the
\emph{class-uniformized chain} of $y$ (start uniform on $\Omega_0$, resample uniformly inside the current class before each step, move, kill
outside $\Omega_t$), and $\kappa_\beta(y)$ the fraction of $\Omega_0$ whose trajectory under the $\beta$'s is killed. For every $y$ and $z>0$,
\[
\Pr_\beta\{\kappa_\beta(y)\ge\kappa_{\rm class}(y)+z\}\le\exp(-2z^2|\Omega_0|/n),
\]
so with probability at least $\frac12$, $\max_y\kappa_\beta(y)\le\max_y\kappa_{\rm class}(y)+\sqrt{n(n\ln r+\ln2)/(2|\Omega_0|)}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{definition}[The balance condition]\label{def:balance}
A free exact permutation round with bath $p$, of entropy $S$ and entropy production $a$, satisfies the \emph{balance condition} at $(n,\epsilon)$ with
exponent $\kappa$ if there are windows $\Omega_0\subseteq\dots\subseteq\Omega_n$ on which the round is triangle-good and collision-free and partitions of each
$\Omega_{t-1}$ into classes, all fixed in advance, such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(B\arabic*),itemsep=1pt]
\item $|\Omega_0|\ge512n^3(n\ln r+\ln2)/\epsilon^4$;
\item $\log\max_t|\Omega_t|\le(\log(n/\epsilon))^\kappa(1+S+n\,a)$;
\item for every label sequence the class-uniformized chain is killed with probability at most $\epsilon^2/(32n)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The windows and classes may contain slots: a point of $\Omega\times[m_t]$ is a point of $\Omega$ together with a slot that the labels do not move.

\begin{corollary}[Balance streams]\label{cor:balance}
A free exact permutation round that satisfies the balance condition at $(n,\epsilon)$ has a rank-rate service with error at most $0.61\epsilon$ and
$Q\le(\log(n/\epsilon))^\kappa(1+S+n\,a)+O_r(\log(n/\epsilon)+\log\log|\Omega_W|)$, where $\Omega_W$ is the union of the windows of the balance
condition and their images under the slot words. The same holds for a free block-monomial round whose base round, with each $f(g)$
extended to a permutation of $\Omega$ that is triangle-good on the windows, satisfies the balance condition.
\end{corollary}

The given bath enters only through the budget (B2): on the windows every round is the gadget unitary of the round, which is exact for every memory
state there, so the device may start the chain wherever it likes. It must, because the chain cannot start from the given bath.

\begin{proposition}[The start belongs to the device]\label{prop:start}
For the gadget of $\Z$ with labels $1,\sfa,\sfa^{-1}$, and every $n\ge2$, there is a bath $p$ on a cycle $\Z_{N'}$, $N'\ge2^n+3$, all of whose points are
triangle-good and collision-free, with $S+n\,a=O(\log n)$, such that a class-uniformized chain started from $p$ is killed at its first step with
probability at least $\epsilon^2/(32n)$ unless $\log|\Omega_0|\ge n-1$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{The Faber--Krahn form}

For a permutation round the cost $S+n\,a$ of a bath has a geometric meaning. For $\varphi:\Omega\to\R$ put
$\mathcal E(\varphi)=\sum_g\sum_x(\varphi(f(g)x)-\varphi(x))^2$, and let
\[
\Lambda(v)=\inf\Bigl\{\frac{\mathcal E(\varphi)}{\nrm\varphi^2}:\ \varphi\ge0,\ \varphi\ne0,\ |\supp\varphi|\le v\Bigr\}
\]
be the $\ell^2$-isoperimetric profile of the Schreier graph, the Faber--Krahn function of Grigor'yan~\cite{Grigoryan1994}; $\Lambda_{\rm good}$ is the same
infimum over $\varphi$ supported on triangle-good, collision-free points. Put $C_\mu=2\ln2\sum_{g\ne1}(1/\mu_g+1/\mu_1)\le4(r-1)d\ln2$.

\begin{theorem}[The entropic Faber--Krahn bound]\label{thm:FK}
For every permutation round and every bath $p$ supported on triangle-good points:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=1pt]
\item $\mathcal E(\sqrt p)\le C_\mu\,a$;
\item $a\ge\Lambda(2^{2S+4})/(8C_\mu)$, and $a\ge\Lambda_{\rm good}(2^{2S+4})/(8C_\mu)$ when $p$ also lives on collision-free points;
\item conversely, every $\varphi\ge0$ on the good points with $\nrm\varphi=1$ and $|\supp\varphi|\le v$ gives the bath $p=\varphi^2$ of an exact round with
$S\le\log v$ and $a\le(16r/\ln2)\,\mathcal E(\varphi)$.
\end{enumerate}
Hence, writing $L^{\rm perm}(n)$ for the least $S+n\,a$ over zero-leakage permutation rounds on $(\Omega,f)$ whose baths live on triangle-good,
collision-free points,
\[
\min_S\Bigl[S+\frac{n\,\Lambda_{\rm good}(2^{2S+4})}{8C_\mu}\Bigr]\ \le\ L^{\rm perm}(n)\ \le\ \min_v\Bigl[\log v+\frac{16r}{\ln2}\,n\,\Lambda_{\rm good}(v)\Bigr].
\]
\end{theorem}

The two-scale cost of permutation rounds is therefore the Faber--Krahn profile of the Schreier graph, up to constants and the change $v\mapsto16v^2$ of
the argument (compare~\cite[Proposition~3.14]{Cornulier2013}, which relates sofic and isoperimetric profiles); the balance condition asks for a device whose windows cost $\mathrm{polylog}\cdot\min_v[\log v+n\Lambda(v)]$. The flat bath of a level
set pays the $\ell^1$ (Cheeger) profile instead, which is the square root of the $\ell^2$ one in the worst case. On groups of exponential growth the
Faber--Krahn profile is large.

\begin{proposition}[Exponential growth costs $n^{1/3}$]\label{prop:growth}
Let the labels generate a group whose balls in $\cL\cup\cL^{-1}$ satisfy $|B(\varrho)|\ge\omega_0^\varrho$ for some $\omega_0>1$, and put
$\phi(v)=\min\{\varrho:|B(\varrho)|\ge v\}$. Consider a permutation round in which, for some choice of geodesic words $w_x$, of length $|x|$, for the elements $x$ of
$B(\varrho_*)$, $\varrho_*=\phi(2^{2S+5})$, the points $f(w_x)y$ are distinct for every $y\in\supp p$, and $p$ is supported on triangle-good points (both hold for the regular action). Then
\[
a\ge\frac1{128\,r\,C_\mu\,\phi(2^{2S+5})^2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad S+n\,a\ge\frac3{2^{2/3}}\Bigl(\frac c{\alpha^2}\Bigr)^{1/3}n^{1/3}-\frac{\beta_0}\alpha,
\]
with $c=1/(128rC_\mu)$, $\alpha=2/\log\omega_0$ and $\beta_0=5/\log\omega_0+1$.
\end{proposition}

The first step of the proof is the isoperimetric inequality of Coulhon and Saloff-Coste~\cite[Th\'eor\`eme~1]{CoulhonSaloffCoste1993}, for the
Schreier graph near the bath; a co-area argument then passes from $\ell^1$ to $\ell^2$.

\subsection{Devices that satisfy the balance condition}

\paragraph{Re-folding.} The basic device sorts its units by distance from a centre and refills the inner positions first, while the number of
slots per point grows slowly. Points $c+(\Z+\frac12)$ around a half-integer centre $c$ carry $m_t$ slots at time $t$, with
$m_t=\lceil(1+\gamma)m_{t-1}\rceil$, so that $\gamma_t:=m_t/m_{t-1}-1\ge\gamma$; the classes are the pairs $\{c\pm D\}$ times the slots. After the round
the device shifts by a fixed integer and \emph{re-folds by distance}: the units are ranked by distance from $c$, the two points of a pair
interleaved, and the unit of rank $N$ goes to the pair $\lfloor N/(2m_t)\rfloor$; units beyond the window are discarded.

\begin{lemma}[The re-folding chain]\label{lem:refold}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=1pt]
\item Under independent class-uniform permutations, the distance $D_t$ of the branch of a fixed label sequence with integer steps $\delta_t$ is the
Markov chain $X=|D+\sigma\delta_t|$ with a uniform sign $\sigma$, followed by $D'=X-B$ with $B\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}((X-\frac12)\gamma_t)$ if
$(X-\frac12)\gamma_t\le1$, and $D'\le X-1$ otherwise.
\item Let $|\delta_t|\le\delta_{\max}$ ($\delta_{\max}\ge1$), $D_0\le R_0$, $0<\eta<1$, $L=\ln(n(1+8/\gamma)/\eta)$ and $\gamma L\le1/(2\delta_{\max}^2)$. Then
\[
\Pr\bigl(\exists t\le n:\ D_t\ge R_0+2\delta_{\max}\sqrt{2L/\gamma}+2\delta_{\max}+\tfrac12\bigr)\le\eta .
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

The spread is set by the capacity growth $\gamma$, not by the shape of the bath: the chain has stationary distance about $|\delta|/\sqrt{2\gamma}$, and
no automorphism of the group is used.

\begin{theorem}[Fibred translation devices]\label{thm:fibred}
Let a gadget channel have its relations in a group $G$ in which its labels are distinct, acting on itself by the regular action. Let
$\Omega_B\subseteq G$ carry a coordinate map $\pi:\Omega_B\to\Z^k$ whose fibres have a common size $\Phi_f$, let the slot words have integer
translations $\pi(w)$, and let $B\subset\Z^k$ be a box such that for every $v\in B$ and every slot word $w$ the action of $w$ maps the fibre over $v$
bijectively onto the fibre over $v-\pi(w)$, which lies in $\Omega_B$. Let $r_0\in\Z^k$, $\delta_i=\max_g|\pi_i(g)-r_{0,i}|$, $\delta_{\max}=\max_i\delta_i\ge1$,
and let the window $W_x=\prod_i[c_i-x_i,c_i+x_i]\subseteq B$ have half-integer centres and $x_i=1+2\delta_i+2\delta_i\sqrt{2L/\gamma}$, where $n\ge2$,
$0<\epsilon\le\frac1{16}$, $0<\gamma<1$, $0<\eta\le\min(\epsilon^2/(64n),1/(4n^3))$, $L=\ln(kn(1+8/\gamma)/\eta)$ and $\gamma L\le1/(2\delta_{\max}^2)$. Let
$\Omega_W$ be $\pi^{-1}(W_x)$ together with its images under the slot words. Then the channel has a rank-rate service with $C=0$ and error at most
$\epsilon$ in which every round acts on the window as the gadget unitary of the regular action, the memory has mass at most $3/(4n^2)$ outside the
window at the start of every round, for every observer, and
\[
Q\le\log|\Omega_W|+nk\log(1+\gamma)+O_{r,k}\bigl(\log(n/\epsilon)+\log\log|\Omega_W|\bigr).
\]
For $\Z^k$ (trivial fibres, $\gamma=1/n$) this is $O_{k,r}(\log(n/\epsilon))$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Collars.} The same mechanism works on any Schreier graph that has nested sets with balanced shells. Let
$F_0\subset F_1\subset\dots\subset F_{K+1}\subseteq\Omega$ satisfy $f(g)F_j\subseteq F_{j+1}$ and $f(g)^{-1}F_j\subseteq F_{j+1}$ for every label and $j\le K$; put
$L_j=F_j\setminus F_{j-1}$ (the \emph{shells}, $L_0=F_0$), $s_j=|L_j|$, $V_j=|F_j|$ and the cut fluxes $\Phi_g(j)=|f(g)F_j\setminus F_j|$. This is a
\emph{collar} of depth $K$.

\begin{proposition}[Collar devices]\label{prop:collar}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=1pt]
\item For $x$ uniform on $L_j$ ($j\ge1$), $f(g)x\in L_{j-1}\cup L_j\cup L_{j+1}$, and $s_jp_+(j)=\Phi_g(j)$, $s_jp_-(j)=\Phi_g(j-1)$: for every label the
shell index is a birth--death chain, reversible for the shell sizes, with drift $(\Phi_g(j)-\Phi_g(j-1))/s_j$.
\item Use the classes $\{F_0,L_1,\dots,L_K\}$ times the slots, random class permutations, point-block twirls, the round, and the \emph{volume
re-fold} (units ranked shell by shell and slot by slot inside a shell, ranks kept, units of rank at least $V_Km_t$ discarded). Suppose
$s_j\in[s,2s]$ for $1\le j\le K$, $(\Phi_g(j)-\Phi_g(j-1))_+\le\zeta s$, $|F_K\setminus F_0|\le|F_0|$, $\zeta\le L_c/K$, $K\ge8L_c$ and $m_0\ge\max(n,4)$,
where $L_c=\ln(n(1+8K^2)/\eta)$. Then $\gamma=2s(2\zeta+4L_c/K+1/m_0)/|F_0|$ gives kill probability at most $\eta$ for every label sequence
of the class-uniformized chain started in the core. With $\eta=\min(\epsilon^2/(64n),1/(4n^3))$, random class permutations and
Theorem~\ref{thm:pointblock}, this is a rank-rate service with error at most $\epsilon$ and memory
\[
Q\le\log|F_{K+1}|+17.4\,n\,L_c/K^2+O_r\bigl(\log(n/\epsilon)+\log\log|F_{K+1}|\bigr).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The drift of each label is the discrete derivative of its boundary flux, so balanced shells make every label diffusive. For the regular action
of $\Z_m\wr\Z$, with labels that move the position by at most one and toggle only at the current position, the sets of configurations with lamps in
$[-2K,2K]$ and position in $[-K-j,K+j]$ form a collar with constant shells and fluxes, and the cost is
$\min_K[(4K+1)\log m+17.4nL_c/K^2]=O(n^{1/3}(\log m)^{2/3}L_c^{1/3})$.

\begin{theorem}[The collar condition]\label{thm:collar-condition}
Say that $(\Omega,f)$ satisfies the \emph{collar condition} with constant $\kappa$ if there are collars inside the good, collision-free points with
shell sizes $s_j\in[s,2s]$, flux increments $(\Phi_g(j)-\Phi_g(j-1))_+\le\zeta s$, $|F_K\setminus F_0|\le|F_0|$ and $\zeta\le1/K$ such that (a) for every $v\ge2$ there is one of depth $K$ with
$\log|F_{K+1}|\le\kappa(1+\log v)(\log(2+\log v))^\kappa$ and $K^{-2}\le\kappa(\log(2+\log v))^\kappa\Lambda(v)$, and (b) for every $K_0$ there is one of
depth at least $K_0$ with $\log|F_{K+1}|\le\kappa K_0^\kappa$. Then, with the devices of Proposition~\ref{prop:collar}(b) run at $m_0\ge\max(n,4)$, every bath on $(\Omega,f)$ satisfies the balance
condition with windows of size
\[
\log\max_t|\Omega_t|\le\bigl(\log(n/\epsilon)+\log(2+S)\bigr)^{\kappa'}(1+S+n\,a),\qquad \kappa'=\kappa'(\kappa),\ n\ge n_0 .
\]
For the regular action of a group of exponential growth, collars of size $e^{O(K\log K)}$ at every depth $K$ suffice.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Houghton groups]\label{thm:houghton-groups}
Let $q\ge3$ and let a gadget channel have its relations in Houghton's group $H_q$ and its labels distinct there, given by slot words of length at
most $\ell$ whose translation parts are also bounded by $\ell$. The implied constants below depend on $q$, on $d$ and $r$, on a bound $\varrho_0$ for the defect
radii $\varrho(w^{-1})$ of the inverse slot words, defined in the proof, and on the growth rate of the label subgroup. There is a rank-rate service with error at most $\epsilon$ in which every round acts on a window of the regular action of $H_q$, with
memory mass at most $3/(4n^2)$ outside it, and
\[
Q=O\bigl(q^{5/3}(\ell^2L)^{1/3}n^{1/3}(\log n)^{2/3}+q^2\ell^2L\log(q\ell L)\bigr)+O(\log(n/\epsilon)),\qquad L=O(\log(n/\epsilon)).
\]
If the labels generate a subgroup of exponential growth, every bath on the regular action has $S+n\,a\ge c_qn^{1/3}-C_q$, so
$Q\le K\log^2(n/\epsilon)(1+S+n\,a)$ for $n\ge n_0$: the regular-action rounds of the channel stream. Otherwise the channel has
$\Qs(n,\epsilon)=O(\log(n/\epsilon))$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:map}.
\end{theorem}

The window comes from a coarse fibration of $H_q$ by its translation part, whose cocycles stay in a finite symmetric group whose support grows
linearly with the window (Lemma~\ref{lem:houghton-cocycle}); this is the mechanism of the corrected extension
bound~\cite[Proposition~7.1]{DouglasMghirbiA}. The comparison with the bath is Proposition~\ref{prop:growth}. The dichotomy in the
statement is Chou's theorem: a finitely generated elementary amenable group has polynomial or exponential growth~\cite[Theorem~3.2]{Chou1980},
and in the first case it is virtually nilpotent, hence residually finite.

\begin{proposition}[The ball device, and the configuration-lamp group of~\cite{DouglasMghirbiC}]\label{prop:ball}
On a regular action, the device that starts at a point $x_0$, uses the word balls $\Omega_t=\{f(w)x_0:|w|\le t\}$ as windows and point classes, and
never re-folds, never kills a branch; Theorem~\ref{thm:pointblock} gives error at most $0.53\epsilon$ and $Q\le n\log(2r+1)+O_r(\log(n/\epsilon))$. For
the configuration-lamp group $\Gamma$ of~\cite{DouglasMghirbiC}, every bath on the regular action has $S+n\,a\ge c'n/(\log n)^2$, so the ball device streams every
regular-action round of the configuration-lamp channel.
\end{proposition}

The cheap rounds of the configuration-lamp channel are not regular-action rounds: its sofic models give $\Ls(n)\le n^{1/2+o(1)}$, and the law holds for it by
Corollary~\ref{cor:lamp} with no device of this section. The configuration-lamp group also shows that the regular action can be far from the cheapest one,
$n/(\log n)^2$ against $n^{1/2+o(1)}$.

\subsection{Leaky biased rounds}

\begin{theorem}[Leaky permutation rounds]\label{thm:perm-leaky}
Let a permutation round have any diagonal bath $p$, distance $u\le\frac1{16}$, leakage $\ell\le C_0/n$ and $n\ge64dC_0$. There is a free exact
permutation round, on the product model $f^{\times t}$ with $t=\lceil\log_{15/4}(r^2n^2/2)\rceil$ and a bath on triangle-good, collision-free
tuples, with distance $0$,
\[
S'\le\frac{tS}{(1-\nu_1)(1-\nu_2)},\qquad n\,a'\le(1+O(\nu_1))\,t\,n\,a+O(t\,d\,C_0\log n)+O(1),
\]
where $\nu_1\le4d\ell\le\frac1{16}$ and $\nu_2\le1/n^2$. So $S'+n\,a'\le O(\log(rn))(1+S+n\,a+dC_0\log n)$, and the round streams if the output
satisfies the balance condition with windows inside the good, collision-free tuples.
\end{theorem}

For general biased rounds we need a rounding that works for every bath. Write $W=U(I\otimes\iota_K)=G+L$ for the restriction of the round to its
support, with $G=\sum_gA_g\otimes X_g$ the Hilbert--Schmidt projection onto the Kraus operators and $L$ the rest, and call $\Lambda=\Tr_d(L^*L)$ on $K$ the
\emph{leak operator}; then $\ell=\Tr(\rho\Lambda)/d$.

\begin{lemma}[Entropy production is a conditional entropy]\label{lem:conditional}
For every round and every state $\sigma$ on $K$, $a(\sigma)=H(C|R)$ for the state of $C$, the output together with the reference of a maximally
entangled input, and $R$, a purifying reference of $\sigma$. If the round is exact on $K$ with gadget family $B_g$, then $a(\sigma)=H(L|R)$ for the
state $\sum_{g,h}\sqrt{\mu_g\mu_h}\ket g\bra h\otimes\sqrt\sigma B_g^*B_h\sqrt\sigma$ on $\C^r\otimes K$, whose $K$-marginal is $\sigma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Operator domination of the defects]\label{lem:domination}
If $W=G+L$ is an isometry and $0<t<1$, then $\pm(G^*G-I)\le(tI+(1+1/t)L^*L)/(1-t)$. For a gadget round, with $\beta_t=(tI+(1+1/t)\Lambda)/(1-t)$ on $K$,
every diagonal defect satisfies $-\beta_t\le X_g^*X_g-I\le\beta_t$, and every triangle defect $R_t=X_1^*X_{[y]}-X_{[x]}^*X_{[z]}$ satisfies
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2\beta_t&R_t\\R_t^*&2\beta_t\end{smallmatrix}\right)\ge0$.
\end{lemma}

Every defect of a leaky round is thus dominated in operator order by one operator built from the leak. The rounding puts the correction that this
domination requires into the bath rather than into the family.

\begin{theorem}[Biased first-order rounding]\label{thm:BF}
Let $\Phi$ be a gadget channel and $(U,\rho)$ a round with any finite bath, distance $u$, leakage $\ell$, entropy production $a$ and bath entropy $S$,
with $s:=\sqrt{d\ell}\le1/(46c_r)$, where $c_r=1+2(r-1)(r^2-1)$. There is a zero-leakage round of $\Phi$ with
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),itemsep=1pt]
\item weighted Gram off-diagonals at most $2u(1+1.05c_rs)+4.2c_rs$, which is at most $\frac14$ when $u\le\frac1{16}$;
\item bath entropy $S'\le(2+3.4c_rs)S+3.1c_rs$;
\item entropy production $a'\le\dfrac{a+h_2(\nu)}{(1-\nu)(1-\ell_0)}+\dfrac{h_2(\ell_0)+\ell_0\log(d^2-r)}{1-\ell_0}+2p\log r+h_2(p)$, with $\nu\le c_rs$,
$\ell_0\le\ell/(1-\nu)$ and $p\le2.2c_rs$.
\end{enumerate}
At the scale of the law: if $\ell\le c_1/n$ and $n\ge n_{BF}:=\lceil2116c_r^2dc_1\rceil$, then $S'\le2.08S+0.07$ and
$n\,a'\le(1+O(n^{-1/2}))\,n\,a+O_{d,r,c_1}(\sqrt n\log n)$.
\end{theorem}

The construction restricts the bath to the eigenvectors of $\rho$ on which the leak is small (a restriction that commutes with $\rho$), dominates
every defect by $\beta_s$, projects the off-diagonal blocks onto the identities, fills the diagonal, and absorbs the correction into the bath,
$\rho'=(\rho_0+\sqrt{\rho_0}Z\sqrt{\rho_0})/(1+z)$. The new label--bath state of Lemma~\ref{lem:conditional} is a mixture of the old Kraus part and a
positive correction of weight $p$, so the entropy production moves linearly in $\sqrt\ell$, and the bath entropy at most doubles. No bath dimension,
spread or truncation enters.

\begin{corollary}[The upper half up to an additive $\sqrt n\log n$]\label{cor:BF}
For a canonical untwisted anchored gadget, for $n\ge\max(n_0,n_{BF})$ (with
$c_1=\frac2{15}$) and $0<\epsilon\le\frac1{16}$,
\[
\frac{\Ls(n)-13.6-0.14\log n}{7.4}\le\Qs(n,\epsilon)\le C_{d,r}\log_2^2\!\left(\frac{128(n+1)}\epsilon\right)\bigl(1+\Ls(n)+\sqrt n\log n\bigr).
\]
\end{corollary}

The upper has $C=0$, $P\le Q$ and the stated rank-rate exchange. Thus the two-scale law holds for every such gadget with $\Ls(n)\ge\sqrt n/\mathrm{polylog}(n)$,
whatever its optimal rounds look like. The proofs of this section are in Appendix~\ref{app:biased}.
