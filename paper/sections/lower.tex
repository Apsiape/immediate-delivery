\section{Extracting a one-round memory lower bound}\label{sec:lower}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Two scales in one round}

The lower half rests on a single observation about the proof of sequential extraction~\cite[Theorem~5.2]{DouglasMghirbiB}: it bounds the
leakage and the entropy production of the extracted round separately, and the two bounds live at different scales. Leakage is a
probability of failing a support test, so the error budget $\epsilon$ controls its sum over the rounds; entropy production is an increase of
memory entropy, so the purity budget controls its sum.

\begin{theorem}[Two-scale extraction]\label{thm:extraction}
Let a causal service of $\Phi$ for $n$ rounds have memory $Q$, error $\epsilon<\frac12$ and purity $P+C\le B$, and put
$B'=B+h_2(\epsilon)+\epsilon\log n$. For every $\lambda>1$ some round $t$ gives a round $(U_t,\rho_t)$ of $\Phi$, where $U_t$ is the unitary of
round $t$ on the input and the $Q$ memory qubits and $\rho_t$ is a memory state, with bath dimension $2^Q$ and
\[
\ddia(\Psi_t,\Phi)\le\epsilon,\qquad \ell_t\le\frac{\lambda\,\epsilon}{n(1-\epsilon)},\qquad a_t\le\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}\cdot\frac{B'}{n(1-\epsilon)} .
\]
\end{theorem}

The state $\rho_t$ is the memory state conditioned on an observer passing the support tests of rounds $1,\dots,t-1$. The joint budget
$\ell_t+a_t\le(B+\epsilon+h_2(\epsilon)+\epsilon\log n)/(n(1-\epsilon))$ of~\cite[Theorem~5.2]{DouglasMghirbiB} follows from the two budgets
established in its proof; the split is what is new. At the rank rate the purity is paid for with memory.

\begin{proposition}[Purity budget~{\cite[Proposition~13.1]{DouglasMghirbiC}}]\label{prop:purity}
If $\Phi$ has a flat probe of rank $r$, every causal service of $\Phi$ for $n$ rounds with error $\epsilon\le\frac1{16}$ and exchange
$J\le\frac n2\log r$ has $P+C<3Q+6$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[The memory lower bound]\label{thm:lower}
Let $\Phi$ have a flat probe of rank $r$. A rank-rate service
with $n\geq1$ rounds and error $\epsilon\leq1/16$ has a static
round of bath dimension $2^Q$, distance at most $\epsilon$,
leakage at most $32\epsilon/(15n)$, and
$n a\leq C Q+C'\log_2(en)$, for universal constants.
Consequently, for universal $c,C''>0$,
\[
 Q\geq c L_\Phi(n,\epsilon)-C''\log_2(en)
 \geq c L^*_\Phi(n)-C''\log_2(en).
\]
At arbitrary exchange, Theorem~\ref{thm:extraction} retains the
separate leakage and supplied-purity budgets. The detailed
inequalities are in Theorem~\ref{thm:lower-constants}.
\end{theorem}

The profile does not depend on $\epsilon$: one function $\Ls$ bounds the memory at every error up to $\frac1{16}$. Under the single budget
of~\cite[Theorem~5.2]{DouglasMghirbiB} the leakage would be read at the purity scale $(Q+\log n)/n$; for Houghton's channel that caps the method at
$n^{1/4}$, while the two-scale profile can reach $n^{1/3}$ (Remark~\ref{rem:quarter} and Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling-houghton}).

\begin{remark}[The extracted round is a round to be streamed]\label{rem:same-class}
The round of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} has an arbitrary bath state on $Q$ qubits, distance at most $\frac1{16}$, leakage at most $\frac2{15n}$,
$S\le Q$ and $n\,a=O(Q+\log(en))$. It belongs to the class of rounds over which $\Ls(n)$ is defined, at the same scales. So an upper
bound $\Qs(n,\epsilon)\le K(\log(n/\epsilon))^\kappa(1+\Ls(n))$ follows if all these rounds stream in the following sense; no rescaling of $\epsilon$ is needed
to match the two halves.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Streaming]\label{def:streaming}
A class $\mathcal C$ of rounds of $\Phi$ \emph{streams} if there are $K,\kappa,n_0$ such that for all $n\ge n_0$ and $0<\epsilon\le\frac1{16}$,
every round of $\mathcal C$ with distance at most $\frac1{16}$ and leakage at most $\frac2{15n}$ gives a rank-rate service of $\Phi$ for $n$ rounds
with error at most $\epsilon$ and memory
\[
Q\le K\bigl(\log(n/\epsilon)\bigr)^\kappa\bigl(1+S+n\,a\bigr).
\]
It streams \emph{up to an additive term} $E(n)$ if the same holds with $S+n\,a+E(n)$ in place of $S+n\,a$.
\end{definition}

If the class of all rounds streams, then $\Qs(n,\epsilon)\le K(\log(n/\epsilon))^\kappa(1+\Ls(n)+1)$, which together with
Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} is the two-scale law. Conversely, if the law holds then every class streams, since the service need not come from the
round. Rounds with $a\ge(\log(n/\epsilon))^{-\kappa}$ always stream with $K\geq\lceil\log r\rceil$: for them the linear
ceiling of Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} gives $n\lceil\log r\rceil\le\lceil\log r\rceil(\log(n/\epsilon))^\kappa\,n\,a$.

\subsection{The rank form}

The upper half of~\cite[Theorem~12.2]{DouglasMghirbiC} streams exact rectangular dilations at the cost $\log k+n\log(k'/k)$, which is the
cost of the round they give (Lemma~\ref{lem:dilation}(c)) with the entropy production replaced by the room. Its device reads only the flat
Gram of the family, so it tolerates label traces that are merely bounded away from one.

\begin{theorem}[Robust streaming]\label{thm:robust}
Let $\Phi$ have a flat probe of rank $r$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=1pt]
\item Let $W=\sum_iA_i\otimes B_i:\C^d\otimes\C^k\to\C^d\otimes\C^{k'}$ be an isometry, with no condition on the traces, and put
$G_{ij}=\tau_k(B_i^*B_j)$. For $n\ge2$, $0<\epsilon<1$ and $n\nrm{G-I}\le1$ there is a causal service of $\Phi$ for $n$ rounds with $C=0$,
$J=\lfloor\frac{n-1}2\log r\rfloor$, $P\le n\log(k'/k)+2$,
\[
Q\le\log k+n\log(k'/k)+O_r\bigl(\log(n/\epsilon)+\log(2+\log k)\bigr),\qquad\text{error}\le e^2\epsilon+\tfrac n2\nrm{G-I}.
\]
\item For a gadget channel, if $(B_g)$ is a gadget family then so is $(B_g^{\otimes t})$, with room $t\log(k'/k)$, dimension $k^t$ and flat Gram
entries $\tau_k(B_g^*B_h)^t$.
\item For a gadget channel, let $(B_g)$ be a gadget family with room $\delta$ and flat Gram off-diagonals at most $c<1$, and put
$t=\lceil\log(2rn/\epsilon)/\log(1/c)\rceil$. Then, for $n\ge2$, there is a rank-rate service with error at most $(e^2+1)\epsilon$, $C=0$,
$P\le nt\delta+2$ and $Q\le t\log k+nt\delta+O_r(\log(n/\epsilon)+\log(2+t\log k))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[The rank-form sandwich]\label{cor:sandwich}
If $\Phi$ has a flat probe, then for $n\ge2$ and $0<\epsilon\le\frac1{16}$,
\[
c L_\Phi(n,\epsilon)-C\log_2(en)\ \le\ \Qs(n,\epsilon)\ \le\ U_\Phi(n)+K_r\bigl(\log(n/\epsilon)+\log(2+U_\Phi(n))\bigr),
\]
and $L_\Phi(n,\epsilon)\le U_\Phi(n)$.
\end{corollary}

The gap between the two sides is a gap between Shannon and rank entropy, and between a biased and a flat bath. When the labels are separated
in finite dimension it closes trivially.

\begin{corollary}[Labels separated in finite dimension]\label{cor:map}
Let $\Phi$ be a gadget channel. If some finite-dimensional unitary representation of $P_\Phi$ separates the labels, then
$\Qs(n,\epsilon)=O_\Phi(\log(n/\epsilon))$.
\end{corollary}

This covers every gadget channel whose labels are distinct in $\Z^k$, in a residually finite group or in a maximally almost periodic group in
which the relations hold. Houghton's channel is not of this kind: every finite-dimensional unitary representation of $H_3$ is trivial on the
finitary alternating group, by Jordan's theorem on finite subgroups of $U(D)$~\cite{Jordan1878}, so it identifies a label that is a
three-cycle with the label $1$ (Remark~\ref{rem:houghton-map}).

\subsection{The configuration-lamp class}

The concrete group in~\cite[Section~3]{DouglasMghirbiC} is the
permutational wreath product
\[
 \Gamma=S_3\wr_V\mathrm{A}=\Bigl(\bigoplus_{v\in V}S_3\Bigr)\rtimes\mathrm{A},\qquad
 \mathrm{A}=V\rtimes(\mathrm{GL}_{\rm fin}(Y)H_3),\qquad
 V=\mathbb F_2^{(Y)},\quad Y=\{1,2,3\}\times\mathbb N .
\]
Here $\mathrm{GL}_{\rm fin}(Y)$ consists of the linear maps of $V$ acting
as the identity off finitely many coordinates, and $H_3$ permutes the
coordinates through its action on $Y$. The affine group $\mathrm{A}$
acts on $V$ by translations and these linear maps. A lamp $S_3$ sits at
each point of $V$, a finite binary \emph{configuration}, and
$\mathrm{A}$ permutes the lamps. The group $\Gamma$ is finitely
presented, generated by five elements, and elementary
amenable~\cite[Theorem~A]{DouglasMghirbiC}. This is the
configuration-lamp group and channel throughout this paper. It differs from the usual
binary lamplighter $C_2\wr\mathbb Z$, whose finite quotients
separate finite label sets (reduce the shift and lamp locations
modulo a sufficiently large integer). A canonical gadget from
that usual lamplighter lies in the logarithmic upper branch.

For the channels of~\cite{DouglasMghirbiC} the two sides already meet. Let $\Phi$ be a gadget channel that satisfies the
hypotheses of~\cite[Theorem~10.1]{DouglasMghirbiC}; its generators include the transpositions $\sfa,\sfb$ of one lamp $S_3$.
Suppose that for every $k\geq1$ there are $2^k$ transport words $u_1,\dots,u_{2^k}$ of length at most $L_k$ whose
\emph{pairwise areas} are at most $A_k$: each commutator $[u_ixu_i^{-1},u_{i'}yu_{i'}^{-1}]$ with $i\ne i'$ and
$x,y\in\{\sfa,\sfb\}$ is a product of at most $A_k$ conjugates of relators and their inverses. Suppose that
$A_k+L_k+1\le Kk^\kappa$, as in~\cite[Corollary~10.2(a)]{DouglasMghirbiC}, and that the chunk $E$ of labels in $\Gamma$
used by these transport words has sofic profile $\log\sigma_E(s)\le C_Es(\log s)^\lambda$.

\begin{corollary}[The configuration-lamp class]\label{cor:lamp}
For such a channel there are $c,C,K,n_0$ with, for $n\ge n_0$ and $0<\epsilon\le\frac1{16}$,
\[
\Ls(n)\ \ge\ \frac{c\sqrt n}{(\log n)^\kappa},\qquad U_\Phi(n)\le C\sqrt n(\log n)^\lambda,\qquad
\Qs(n,\epsilon)\le K\bigl(\log(n/\epsilon)\bigr)^{\kappa+\lambda+1}\bigl(1+\Ls(n)\bigr).
\]
So the two-scale law holds. For the configuration-lamp channel of~\cite[Section~11]{DouglasMghirbiC}, $\Ls(n)$, $\Qs(n,\epsilon)$ and $U_\Phi(n)$ are all
$n^{1/2+o(1)}$; for $\Qs$ this is~\cite[Theorem~11.1(b) and Corollary~12.3]{DouglasMghirbiC} at $\epsilon=\frac1{16}$, and for smaller fixed
$\epsilon$ the upper bound comes from~\cite[Theorem~12.2]{DouglasMghirbiC}, as in the proof of Corollary~12.3 there.
\end{corollary}

The exponent $\frac12$ of~\cite[Theorem~11.1(b)]{DouglasMghirbiC} is therefore not a feature of the method: it is the two-scale profile of the
channel, which balances a bath of entropy $S$ against entropy production of order $1/S$. Theorems~\ref{thm:extraction}--\ref{thm:lower},
Theorem~\ref{thm:robust} and Corollaries~\ref{cor:sandwich}--\ref{cor:lamp} are proved in Appendix~\ref{app:lower}.

This square-root memory statement allows initial purity up to the memory. With a purity budget $B$,
$\log n\le B\le c_0n$, the same channel needs memory of order $n/B$ up to logarithmic factors, matched by exact
services~\cite[Theorem~11.1(a)]{DouglasMghirbiC}; at logarithmic purity this is nearly linear.
