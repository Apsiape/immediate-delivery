\section{A sharp qutrit supplied-purity rate}\label{sec:qutrit-purity}

The general nonclosure converse measures distance to a tracial closure.
For the qutrit Werner--Holevo channel~\cite{WernerHolevo2002} a stronger converse measures an
entire bit rate. Put
\[
 \Theta(X)=\tfrac12(\Tr(X)I-X^T),\qquad H=\log_2 3,
 \qquad g_D(t)=2t\log_2D+(1+t)h_2\!\left(\frac{t}{1+t}\right).
\]
Its normalized Choi state is the flat state on the three-dimensional
antisymmetric subspace. In this section only, we also permit prescribed,
independent imports and permanent exports during a round. Their timing
and states are fixed independently of the observer and device state,
and their purity is counted in $P+C$. Elsewhere exchange happens only
between rounds.

\begin{theorem}[Qutrit purity, at arbitrary exchange timing]\label{thm:qutrit-purity}
Every immediate-output service of $\Theta$ with complete-experiment
error at most $\epsilon$ satisfies
\[
 P+C\geq n\left[H-g_9\!\left(\min\{1,10\sqrt\epsilon\}\right)\right]_+.
\]
There are exact services with
\[
 Q=O(\sqrt n),\quad P+C=nH+O(\sqrt n+\log n),
 \quad J=nH+O(\sqrt n).
\]
Consequently the optimal asymptotic supplied-purity rate is $H$ when
the error tends to zero, even under the enlarged timing contract.
\end{theorem}

The attained rate equals the purity supplied by one pure three-dimensional
Stinespring environment per round. The lower bound comes from rigidity
(Lemma~\ref{lem:qutrit-rigidity}). The Choi state of $\Theta$ is the flat
state on the three-dimensional antisymmetric subspace of reference and
output, so a round that implements $\Theta$ nearly correctly keeps them
nearly inside that subspace. There, tracing out the output acts as
$\Theta$ itself, which is invertible with stabilized inverse norm at
most $9$. The reference starts independent of a purification of the bath,
so reference and output stay nearly independent of it too. Each use must then raise the bath entropy by
nearly $\log_2 3$ fresh bits, and these gains add up to at most the
supplied purity. The argument holds for every bath dimension and for
memories correlated with earlier outputs. It is a vanishing-error
statement: at coarse error its continuity bound may be vacuous.
The $O(\sqrt n)$ terms of the upper bound come from packing. The pure
qutrit environments of $\lceil\sqrt n\rceil$ consecutive rounds share
one bank of $\lceil\lceil\sqrt n\rceil H\rceil$ qubits, which is exported
when full. Memory holds one bank, and rounding loses less than one qubit
per bank.

Theorem~\ref{thm:qutrit-purity} is the finite-error form of a law that
holds for every channel. For any $\Phi$, the least supplied-purity rate at
vanishing error is the purity cost $\kappa(\Phi)$ of closed repeated
use~\cite[Definition~1.3]{DouglasClosedMemory}, and with memory $o(n)$
the achievable exchange and purity rates $(j,p)$ fill the region
$p\geq\kappa(\Phi)$, $j\geq p$, $2j-p\geq H(\Phi)$, where $H(\Phi)$ is the
largest entropy of a complementary output~\cite[Theorems~3.2 and~3.4]{DouglasMghirbiB}.
The map $\Theta$ is an extreme point of the channels on $M_3$, so
$\kappa(\Theta)=H(\Theta)=H$~\cite[Proposition~F.1(c)]{DouglasMghirbiB},
and the banks above sit at the corner $j=p=H$, where the least purity
and the least exchange are reached together. This section adds the
explicit finite-error bound, the enlarged timing contract, exact services
with memory $O(\sqrt n)$, and the comparison with collective and batched
delivery below. The cost $\kappa$ vanishes exactly on
$\overline{\mathcal F}$~\cite[Theorem~3.2]{DouglasMghirbiB}, so gadgets in
the first two regimes of Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy} have $\kappa=0$ and
those in the linear regime have $\kappa>0$.

\begin{proposition}[The static qutrit gap; Haagerup--Musat~{\cite[Theorem~5.6 and Corollary~5.7]{HaagerupMusat2015}}]\label{prop:qutrit-gap}
In normalized Choi distance,
\[
 \operatorname{dist}_J(\Theta,\overline{\mathcal F})=2/27.
\]
A closest point is $(2/27)W_3^++(25/27)W_3^-$, where $W_3^-=\Theta$ and
$W_3^+(X)=(\Tr(X)I+X^T)/4$.
\end{proposition}

As in~\cite[Section~4 and the proof of Theorem~5.6(2)]{HaagerupMusat2015}, Werner twirling reduces the question to the line
$\lambda W_3^++(1-\lambda)W_3^-$, whose normalized Choi distance from
$\Theta$ is $\lambda$. Haagerup--Musat's factorizability threshold
places the closest factorizable point at
$\lambda=2/27$~\cite[Theorem~5.6 and Corollary~5.7]{HaagerupMusat2015},
and that point has a finite tracial factorization with a three-dimensional
bath~\cite[Theorem~5.6(1)]{HaagerupMusat2015}. The proposition gives an explicit example for Theorem~\ref{thm:closure-law}(b).
The sharper rate theorem uses antisymmetric-support rigidity instead.
Appendix~\ref{app:purity} proves both statements.

\begin{theorem}[Collective qutrit implementation]\label{thm:qutrit-collective}
For $n\geq1$ and $0<\epsilon\leq1/2$, there is one unitary
implementation of $\Theta^{\otimes n}$ with all outputs released
together, half-diamond error at most $\epsilon$, and
\begin{align*}
 Q_{\rm bath}&\leq nH/2+2\log_2(1/\epsilon)+2\log_2(n+1)+O(1),\\
 P&\leq2\log_2(1/\epsilon)+2\log_2(n+1)+O(1),\qquad J=C=0,\\
 Q_{\rm peak}&\leq2n+Q_{\rm bath}+O(1).
\end{align*}
Here $Q_{\rm bath}$ counts the fixed auxiliary bath and
$Q_{\rm peak}$ also counts accepted input/output payload registers
held until delivery. Subexponential inverse error gives
supplied-purity rate zero with linear peak retained memory.
\end{theorem}

The proof applies Lemma~\ref{lem:collective-gaussian}, which holds
for every unital channel. For a fixed unital channel $\Psi$, it gives
an implementation of $\Psi^{\otimes n}$ with all outputs released
together, error $1/\mathrm{poly}(n)$ and supplied purity $O(\log n)$.
At the level of rates this is the zero thermodynamic capacity of
unital channels with trivial Hamiltonians~\cite{FaistBertaBrandao2019},
\cite[Equation~(4.9) and Theorem~7.1]{FaistBertaBrandao2021}. The lemma
adds the explicit $O(\log n)$ term, and Theorem~\ref{thm:qutrit-purity}
shows that immediate delivery raises the rate to $H$.

The collective construction holds the inputs until delivery, so its
memory is linear. The inputs are chosen by the observer, so they are
not independent imports, and releasing them as outputs is not replacement
exchange. Purity is therefore $P=Q_{\rm bath}-S(\beta_{\rm bath})$,
not $Q_{\rm peak}-S(\beta_{\rm bath})$. The same convention applies to
batching with sequential input arrivals.

\begin{theorem}[Logarithmic batching threshold]\label{thm:batch-purity}
Fix contiguous batch lengths $1\leq b_n=o(n)$, complete adaptive error
$n^{-2}$, $Q_{\rm peak}=o(n)$ and $J=O(n)$. Let $R[b]$ be the infimum achievable
$\limsup(P+C)/n$, and put
$x=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\log_2n/b_n\in[0,\infty]$. Then
\[
 H\min\{1,x/\log_2 9\}\leq R[b]\leq\min\{H,6x\}.
\]
In particular $R[b]=H$ for $b_n=o(\log n)$, and $R[b]=0$ if and
only if $b_n/\log n\to\infty$. More generally $R[b]=H$ whenever
$x\geq\log_2 9$, including $b_n\sim c\log_2n$ with
$c\leq1/\log_2 9$. The remaining part of the critical logarithmic
curve is not determined. In the superlogarithmic regime the zero-rate
construction also has $J=nH/2+o(n)$; at critical logarithmic batching
only $J=O(n)$ is asserted.
\end{theorem}

The same logarithmic threshold appears for a closed apparatus serving a
ququart Schur channel on a delivery schedule~\cite[Theorems~7 and~8]{DouglasSchur}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:batch-purity} carries it to open services with exchange.
Its upper half holds for every unital channel, with $H$ replaced by $\log_2r$.

\begin{theorem}[Batching any unital channel]\label{thm:unital-batch}
Let $\Psi$ be a unital channel on $M_d$ with Choi rank $r$. Under the
contract of Theorem~\ref{thm:batch-purity}, let $R_\Psi[b]$ be the
infimum achievable $\limsup(P+C)/n$ for services of $\Psi$, with $x$ as
there. Then
\[
 R_\Psi[b]\leq\min\{\log_2r,\,6x\}.
\]
In particular $R_\Psi[b]=0$ if $b_n/\log n\to\infty$, and the
zero-rate construction then has $J=\tfrac n2\log_2r+o(n)$. With all
outputs released together, rate zero is the zero thermodynamic capacity
of unital channels~\cite{FaistBertaBrandao2019},
\cite[Equation~(4.9) and Theorem~7.1]{FaistBertaBrandao2021}.
\end{theorem}

For $\Theta$, $r=3$ and this is the upper half of
Theorem~\ref{thm:batch-purity}. For a closed apparatus serving one
ququart Schur channel, independent delayed blocks give purity $o(n)$
under superlogarithmic delay~\cite[Theorem~8]{DouglasSchur}. The lower
half has no analogue for all unital channels. A channel with a finite
tracial factorization of bath dimension $M$, such as an equal mixture of
$M$ unitaries, has exact immediate services with $P+C=O(\sqrt n)$,
$Q=O(\sqrt n)$ and $J=n\log_2M+O(\sqrt n)$. Each round uses its own
maximally mixed bath, and the baths are packed in banks as in the proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:qutrit-purity}, so each bank costs less than one
qubit of purity.
