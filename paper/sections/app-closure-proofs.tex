\section{Proofs of the closure law}\label{app:closure-proofs}

This appendix proves the finite-approximation upper and the local
tracialization converse stated in Section~\ref{sec:closure}.
All bath, workspace and exchange counts refer to the service
contract of Section~\ref{sec:model}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:mixture-flat}]
Carath\'eodory's theorem reduces the mixture to $L\le d^4+1$ terms with weights $p_i$ and bath dimensions $m_i\le m$.
Take $\lfloor Np_i/m_i\rfloor$ copies of the $i$th bath block and fill the remaining dimension by identity blocks; this is the block-diagonal
construction in the proof of~\cite[Theorem~3.6]{HaagerupMusat2015}.
The realized weights $q_i=m_i\lfloor Np_i/m_i\rfloor/N$ obey $q_i\le p_i$ and
$1-\sum_iq_i\le Lm/N\le\gamma$. Both the removed mixture and the filler are channels, giving the stated half-diamond bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-repair}]
Choose Hilbert--Schmidt orthogonal minimal Kraus operators $K_i$, with
$\Tr K_i^*K_j/d=\mu_i\delta_{ij}$ and $\mu_i>0$. The flat-probe identity is unchanged by this unitary Kraus change.
The unital completely positive map
\[
 \mathcal A(Y)=\sum_{i,j}K_i^*K_j\otimes Y_{ij}
\]
has a fixed Hermiticity-preserving linear right inverse $\mathcal R$ on its range.
Its amplified trace norm is bounded independently of $k$: writing
$\mathcal R(H)=\sum_j\Tr(F_jH)R_j$ in fixed dual bases, trace duality bounds each amplified coefficient extraction by
$\nrm{F_j}\nrm H_1$, and hence the sum by a fixed constant.

Project the approximant unitary as $U=G+L$, $G=\sum_iK_i\otimes X_i$, with the system coefficients of $L$ orthogonal to the
Kraus span. Its leakage is $\ell=\nrm L_F^2/(dk)\le\delta$.
For $Y^0=[X_i^*X_j]\ge0$, let $H_0=I-G^*G$ and $E=(\mathcal R\otimes\id)(H_0)$.
Here $H_0$ is in the amplified range, and
\[
 \frac{\nrm{H_0}_1}{k}\le d(2\sqrt\ell+\ell),\qquad
 \frac{\nrm E_1}{k}=O_\Phi(\sqrt\ell).
\]
The first inequality follows from $U^*U=I$ and Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy--Schwarz.
Let $Z=r\Tr_{\C^r}E_-\ge0$. The positive pinching inequality gives
$E_-\le I_r\otimes Z$, so
\[
 Y_1=Y^0+E+I_r\otimes Z\ge0,\quad
 \mathcal A(Y_1)=I_d\otimes(I_k+Z).
\]
For $C=(I+Z)^{-1/2}$, the common congruence
$Y=(I_r\otimes C)Y_1(I_r\otimes C)$ is positive and $\mathcal A(Y)=I$.
Factoring $Y$ gives maps $B_i:\C^k\to\C^{rk}$ with $B_i^*B_j=Y_{ij}$, and
$W=\sum_iK_i\otimes B_i$ is a genuine isometry, completed to a unitary on the enlarged bath.

We must bound its entropy without paying $\sqrt\ell\log k$.
Let $D_\mu=\diag(\sqrt{\mu_i})\otimes I_k$.
The output spectrum is that of $\Omega=D_\mu YD_\mu/k$, whose bath-reference marginal is $I_k/k$.
It splits into two positive components,
\[
 \Omega_{\rm old}=D_\mu(I\otimes C)Y^0(I\otimes C)D_\mu/k,\qquad
 \Omega_{\rm corr}=D_\mu(I\otimes C)(E+I\otimes Z)(I\otimes C)D_\mu/k.
\]
The old component factors through the \emph{original square bath}, so its rank is at most $k$; the correction has rank at most $rk$.
Also $\Tr_dH_0=\Tr_dL^*L$, by orthogonality. Thus, with $b=\Tr\Omega_{\rm corr}$,
\[
 0\le b\le\ell+\Tr Z/k=O_\Phi(\sqrt\ell),\qquad
 S(\Omega)\le h_2(b)+(1-b)\log k+b\log(rk).
\]
The repaired entropy production is therefore at most $h_2(b)+b\log r$.
This argument would fail for a general rank-$k$ source in ambient dimension $M$: its old component could have rank $M$,
leaving an extra $(1-b)\log(M/k)$.

The scalar Gram $\bar G_{ij}=\Tr B_i^*B_j/k$ obeys
$\nrm{\bar G-I}=O_\Phi(\sqrt\delta)$ and $\mathcal A(\bar G)=I_d$.
Indeed the original scalar coefficients are bounded fixed linear extractions of the Choi state; the correction has trace norm
$O_\Phi(k\sqrt\ell)$, and the common contraction changes scalar entries by the same order.
The $X_i$ have fixed operator-norm bounds because they are fixed coefficient extractions of a unitary.

Replicate the repaired source to the least positive multiple $K$ of $k$ with $K\ge\delta^{-1/2}$, without changing its normalized Gram or production. Thus $K\le k+\delta^{-1/2}$.
Put $N=\max(1,\lceil K\nrm{\bar G-I}\rceil)$ and
\[
 C_0=I_r-\frac K N(\bar G-I_r)\ge0,\qquad \mathcal A(C_0)=I_d.
\]
If $C_0=[\langle c_i,c_j\rangle]$, the maps $c_i\otimes I_N$ give a correction isometry, with flat-source output entropy
$\log N+S(\sum_i\mu_i\ket{c_i}\bra{c_i})\le\log N+\log r$.
The orthogonal direct sum has source rank $k_1=K+N$, exact scalar Gram
$(K\bar G+NC_0)/(K+N)=I_r$, and production
\[
 a_1\le(1-p)a'+p\log r,\qquad p=N/(K+N)=O_\Phi(\sqrt\delta).
\]
The mixing entropy cancels against $\log(K+N)$. Replication and the added dimension give the logarithmic size bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:generic-upper}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:mixture-flat} turns a mixture approximant at Choi accuracy $\delta$ into a single finite tracial factorization at accuracy
$2\delta$, with $\log k\le\log(D_{\rm mix,\Phi}(\delta)+1)+\log(1/\delta)+O_d(1)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-repair} gives an exact dilation with source rank $k_1$ and production
$a_1\le C_\Phi\sqrt\delta\log(e/\delta)$.

The path compiler of Theorem~\ref{thm:pathreg} applies to this dilation without gadget identities.
For clarity, the hypothesis check is as follows. The flat probe applied to $W^*W=I$ gives
$\sum_iB_i^*B_i=rI$, so $\nrm{B_i}^2\le r$. The flat output bath state is at most $I/k_1$, since $WW^*$ is a projection.
Its top-$k_1$ tail $w$ is at most $a_1\ln2$ by the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:top}, which needs only that spectral ceiling,
not individual isometries $B_i$. Divide the output into at most $r+1$ rank-$k_1$ pieces.
Their matrices $M^{(b)}\ge0$ sum to $I_r$ and induce effects
$V^*(I\otimes M^{(b)})V$, with norm at most $\Tr M^{(b)}$.
Consequently
\[
 \beta=\sum_{b\ge2}w(b)\le a_1\ln2/\mu_{\min}.
\]
Concentration in Lemma~\ref{lem:concentration}(a) uses only $\nrm{B_i}^2\le r$.
The path-type recursion uses positivity and $\sum_bM^{(b)}=I_r$.
Inflow is bounded by $\sum_i C_i^{(b)*}C_i^{(b)}\le rI$, and export uses Lemma~\ref{lem:export}(a).
These are exactly the invariants proved in Appendix~\ref{app:frames}; no group relation or disjoint Kraus support is consumed.

Choose $\bar\beta=C_\Phi\sqrt\delta\log(e/\delta)\le1/4$ and
$\theta=1/\log(1/\bar\beta)$. Then
$u'\le O_r(\bar\beta)$ and
$\log\kappa_\theta/\theta\le O_r(\bar\beta\log(1/\bar\beta))$ by the positive-piece H\"older bound in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:flat-zero}. Insert these in Theorem~\ref{thm:pathreg}.
The target is exact before compilation: $\delta$ contributes to resources, not an $n\delta$ operational error.
Every path register, port and workspace is part of $Q$; the fixed choices of twirls require no runtime random resource.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:closure-law}(a)]
For fixed $\delta>0$ the profile is finite. Divide the preceding bound by $n$ and let $n\to\infty$:
the profile and fixed logarithmic terms vanish, and $\log(n/\epsilon_n)/n\to0$.
Then let $\delta\downarrow0$. Explicitly choose $\delta_j\downarrow0$ so that
$C_\Phi\sqrt{\delta_j}\log^2(e/\delta_j)\le1/j$.
For each $j$ choose increasing thresholds $N_j$ beyond which the remaining terms divided by $n$ are at most $1/j$,
using the fixed finite value $D_{\rm mix,\Phi}(\delta_j)$ and the given error sequence.
Use $\delta_j$ for $N_j\le n<N_{j+1}$. This slow diagonal choice gives $Q/n\le2/j\to0$ and $P/n\le Q/n\to0$.
It asserts no uniform rate of approximation and no logarithmic purity bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:tracialization}]
Let $p_i,q_j$ be the eigenvalues of $\rho,T(\rho)$ and
$w_{ji}=d^{-1}\sum_{s,t}|\langle s,j|U|t,i\rangle|^2$ in their eigenbases.
This is doubly stochastic and $q=wp$. The laws $P_{ji}=w_{ji}p_i$, $R_{ji}=w_{ji}q_j$ satisfy
$D(P\|R)=S(q)-S(p)=a$. Pinsker gives
$\sum_{ji}w_{ji}|p_i-q_j|\le\sqrt{2\ln2\,a}$.
Zeros follow by restriction or continuity.

Sort both spectra decreasingly, matching their eigenvectors by index. The optimal one-dimensional spectral matching gives
$\sum_j|p_j-q_j|\le\sum_{ji}w_{ji}|p_i-q_j|$. Therefore
$\sum_{ji}w_{ji}|p_i-p_j|\le2\sum_{ji}w_{ji}|p_i-q_j|$.
For each $k$, compress $U$ from the top-$k$ input bath subspace to the top-$k$ output bath subspace, identified with $\C^k$.
Its square compression $A=VH$ is a contraction; choose a unitary polar extension $V$.
The unitaries $V(H\pm i\sqrt{I-H^2})$ average to the channel
$X\mapsto AXA^*+BXB^*$, $B=V\sqrt{I-H^2}$, before the flat bath is traced out.
The original flat-source channel is the compressed channel plus a positive outgoing term; the repaired channel is the same compressed
channel plus a positive replacement term. Both terms have normalized Choi trace
$e_k=k^{-1}\sum_{i\le k,j>k}w_{ji}$, so their Choi distance is at most $e_k$.
Now use the layer-cake decomposition
$\rho=\sum_k k(p_k-p_{k+1})P_k/k$, with $p_{M+1}=0$. Its weights sum to one, and the total repair error is at most
\[
 \sum_k(p_k-p_{k+1})\sum_{i\le k,j>k}w_{ji}
 =\sum_{ji}w_{ji}(p_i-p_j)_+
 =\frac12\sum_{ji}w_{ji}|p_i-p_j|.
\]
The last equality uses double stochasticity. The preceding transport bound completes the proof, with finitely many components that are finite tracial factorizations
and bath caps at most $M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:closure-law}(b)]
Use an observer sending fresh Bell inputs and retaining every output and reference, without adaptation.
Even if the memory is correlated with previous outputs, each round's unconditional channel is the round determined by its current memory
marginal. Its Choi marginal is within $\epsilon$ of the target. Its production $a_t\ge0$ obeys
$\sum_ta_t\le B:=P+C$: the deficit $Q-S(\rho_t)$ decreases by $a_t$ at a round, and a replacement raises it by at most the imported deficit.
Lemma~\ref{lem:tracialization}, averaging the repaired channels, and Jensen give
\[
 \Delta\le\epsilon+\sqrt{2\ln2\,B/n}.
\]
It gives $B\ge n(\Delta-\epsilon)_+^2/(2\ln2)$; $P\le Q$ proves (b).
The boundary ledger remains valid with correlations: exporting $E$ from $AE$ and importing an independent state $\beta$
raises the memory deficit by $c+S(E|A)-\log\dim E\le c$, where $c$ is the imported deficit.

An additional converse,~\cite[Corollary~3.3(b)]{DouglasMghirbiB}, remains useful when imported purity is large. Let $H(\Phi)$ be the largest entropy of a minimal complementary output over system inputs. Use a \emph{different} observer sending independent purifications of an entropy-maximizing input.
The ideal retained entropy is $nH(\Phi)$; continuity on dimension at most $d^{2n}$ gives actual entropy at least
$nH(\Phi)-2\epsilon n\log d-1$.
Purifying the device's resources gives an upper bound $2(Q+J)-B$: the complementary system is the final memory, permanent exports
and inaccessible resource purifiers. Both observers test the \emph{same fixed resource ledger}, so at $J\le nH(\Phi)/2$,
$2Q\ge B-2\epsilon n\log d-1$. Combining the inequalities gives, at this entropy-rate exchange budget,
\[
 Q\ge n\left(\frac{(\Delta-\epsilon)_+^2}{4\ln2}-\epsilon\log d\right)-\frac12.
\]

This proof uses imports only between rounds, and the theorem is stated for that case.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:repeatable}]
(a) Feed the maximally mixed state $\tau_d$ at every use. These inputs
are uncorrelated, so the device repeats $\Phi$ on them. Use $j$ is then
the round $(U,\xi_j)$ of Definition~\ref{def:round}. Its channel is
$\Phi$, its bath map $T$ gives $\xi_{j+1}=T(\xi_j)$, and its entropy
production is $a_j=S(\xi_{j+1})-S(\xi_j)\geq0$. The sum telescopes:
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^na_j=S(\xi_{n+1})-S(\xi_1)\leq\log M-S(\xi_1).
\]
Choose $j$ with $a_j\leq(\log M-S(\xi_1))/n$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:tracialization} gives $\Psi\in\operatorname{conv}\mathcal F_M$
with $d_J(\Phi,\Psi)\leq\sqrt{2\ln2\,a_j}$. This is the first
inequality. The second follows from
$\operatorname{conv}\mathcal F_M\subseteq\overline{\mathcal F}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:mixture-flat}). With Bell inputs in place of $\tau_d$
the memory marginals are the same, and each reference and output pair
is exactly in the state $\cJ(\Phi)$. No joint state of several outputs
is used.

(b) For each $n$, the proof of part~(a) gives a round $(U_n,\rho_n)$ on
$\C^d\otimes\C^M$ with channel $\Phi$ and entropy production at most
$(\log M)/n$. Unitaries and states on these fixed spaces form compact
sets, and the channel and the production of a round depend continuously
on it. A limit point $(U,\rho)$ is therefore a round with channel $\Phi$
and production $0$. Let $\omega=U(\tau_d\otimes\rho)U^*$. Then
$S(\omega)=\log d+S(\rho)$, the marginals of $\omega$ are
$\Phi(\tau_d)$ and $T(\rho)$, and $S(T(\rho))=S(\rho)$. Subadditivity
gives $S(\Phi(\tau_d))\geq\log d$. Hence $\Phi(\tau_d)=\tau_d$,
subadditivity is an equality, and $\omega=\tau_d\otimes T(\rho)$, that
is, $U(I\otimes\rho)U^*=I\otimes T(\rho)$. So $T(\rho)=W\rho W^*$ for a
unitary $W$, and $U'=(I\otimes W^*)U$ has the same channel and commutes
with $I\otimes\rho$. Write $\rho=\sum_k\lambda_kP_k$ with distinct
eigenvalues $\lambda_k>0$ and $m_k=\rank P_k$. Then $U'$ restricts to a
unitary $U_k$ on $\C^d\otimes P_k\C^M$. Identifying $P_k\C^M$ with
$\C^{m_k}$,
\[
 \Phi(X)=\sum_k\lambda_k\Tr_{P_k\C^M}U_k(X\otimes P_k)U_k^*
 =\sum_k\lambda_km_k\,(\id\otimes\tau_{m_k})\bigl[U_k(X\otimes I)U_k^*\bigr].
\]
The weights $p_k=\lambda_km_k$ sum to $1$, and
$\sum_km_k=\rank\rho\leq M$.

(c) Use the inputs of part~(a). Each use is a round with channel
$\Theta$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:qutrit-rigidity} with $\delta=0$ gives
$a_j\geq\log3$. The telescoping sum gives $n\log3\leq\log M-S(\xi_1)$.
In the $n$-cell device each cell holds the pure environment of a
three-dimensional Stinespring dilation of $\Theta$. Use $j$ acts on the
input and the first cell and then shifts the cells cyclically, as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}. Each use $j\leq n$ meets a fresh pure
cell, so the device repeats $\Theta$ for $n$ uses, with $M=3^n$ and
$S(\xi_1)=0$. Letting $n\to\infty$ shows that no finite device repeats
$\Theta$.

(d) The $v_k$ are unit vectors, so the $w_k$ are Hermitian unitaries
with $\tau_2(w_kw_l)=v_k\cdot v_l=B_{kl}$. Thus $U$ is unitary, $B\geq0$
and $B_{kk}=1$, so $T_B$ is a unital channel. For every input $\rho$,
\[
 \Tr_{\C^6}U(\rho\otimes\tfrac I2)U^*=\sum_k\rho_{kk}w_k\tfrac I2w_k=\tfrac I2,
 \qquad
 \Tr_{\C^2}U(\rho\otimes\tfrac I2)U^*=\sum_{k,l}\rho_{kl}\tau_2(w_kw_l)\ket k\bra l=T_B(\rho).
\]
This is the catalytic dilation. Haagerup and Musat proved that $T_B$ is
not random-unitary~\cite[Example~3.3]{HaagerupMusat2011}; here is a
direct check. If $T_B=\sum_ip_i\Ad V_i$, then $T_B(\ket k\bra k)=\ket k\bra k$
forces every $V_i$ to be diagonal. So $B=\sum_ip_iz_iz_i^*$ with
unimodular vectors $z_i$, each in the range of $B$ because
$p_iz_iz_i^*\leq B$. Then $z_{i,k}=v_k\cdot c$ for some $c=x+iy\in\C^3$,
so $v_k^T(xx^T+yy^T-I)v_k=0$ for every $k$. A quadratic form vanishing at
$v_1,\dots,v_5$ vanishes on the circle through them, since a nonzero
trigonometric polynomial of degree at most two has at most four zeros;
so it is a multiple of $-4x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2$, and vanishing at $v_0$
makes it zero. Hence $xx^T+yy^T=I$, which is impossible since the left
side has rank at most two.
\end{proof}
