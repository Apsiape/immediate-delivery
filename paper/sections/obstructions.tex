\section{Limits of specified constructions}\label{sec:obstructions}
%======================================================================

This appendix gives method-scoped obstructions: the device of~\cite[Theorem~12.2]{DouglasMghirbiC} cannot reach Shannon cost, a twisted rounding
cannot keep the bath at second order, a rounding cannot keep square families square, and the one-round method of~\cite{DouglasMghirbiB} cannot
certify the Houghton exponent.

\subsection{Global scrambling}

Let $\Phi_\Z$ be the gadget of $\Z$ with labels $1,\sfa,\sfa^{-1}$: $d=5$ and label weights $\mu=(\frac25,\frac3{10},\frac3{10})$.

\begin{theorem}[Global scrambling pays rank room]\label{thm:global-haar}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=1pt]
\item Let $k\ge3$, $U$ the cyclic shift on $\C^k$ and $0<\vartheta<\pi/2$. The maps
\[
B_1=\begin{pmatrix}I\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad B_\sfa=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\vartheta\,U\\\sin\vartheta\,U\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B_{\sfa^{-1}}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\vartheta\,U^*\\-\sin\vartheta\,U^*\end{pmatrix}
\]
form an exact rectangular dilation of $\Phi_\Z$ with $k'=2k$, so its room is exactly one bit, while its flat round has leakage $0$, distance $0$ and
entropy production $a=h_2(0.6\sin^2\vartheta)=\Theta(\vartheta^2\log(1/\vartheta))$.
\item Let $\mathcal A$ be a $D$-dimensional subspace of $\C^k\otimes\C^m$ and $Y_g=(B_g\otimes I)\mathcal A$. Every projection $\Pi$ with
$\Tr(\Pi P_{Y_1})\ge(1-\eta)D$ and $\Tr(\Pi P_{Y_\sfa})\ge(1-\eta)D$ has $\rank\Pi\ge D(2-2\eta/(1-\cos\vartheta))$.
\item Run the device of~\cite[Theorem~12.2]{DouglasMghirbiC} for a gadget channel and an exact rectangular dilation with $q=k'/k$, with independent Haar
unitaries $V_t$ on the active space of dimension $N_{t-1}$, and let $\sigma_t$ be the memory state for the observer that inputs $\tau_d$ in every round.
Then $\E[\Tr\sigma_t^2\mid\sigma_{t-1}]\le s_2\Tr\sigma_{t-1}^2+2(1-s_2)/N_{t-1}$ with $s_2=\sum_g\mu_g^2$. If $qs_2<1$, then for each $t$, with probability at
least $\frac12$ over the Haar unitaries, every projection $\Pi$ with $\Tr(\Pi\sigma_t)\ge1-\eta$ has $\rank\Pi\ge(1-\eta)^2(1-qs_2)D_t/(12q)$, where $D_t$ is
the dimension of the active space after round $t$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

In that device the round-one memory branches forced by anchor inputs are the flat states $P_{Y_g}/D_0$, so by (b) a truncation that keeps a fraction
$1-\eta$ of every forced branch keeps $\log(2-2\eta/(1-\cos\vartheta))$ bits of growth: $0.9997$ bit at $\vartheta=0.1$ and $\eta=10^{-6}$, where
$a=0.053$. By (c) the smoothed rank of the accumulated memory is within $O(1)$ bits of the whole active space. The device reads only the flat Gram
(Theorem~\ref{thm:robust}(a)), re-randomization flattens the bias, and the memory fills the rank-grown space. This blocks the route, not the law:
the round of (a) is an exact flat round, which path registers stream at its Shannon cost (Theorem~\ref{thm:flat-zero}(a)).

\subsection{Rounding at second order}

The following statement was a natural target for the upper half at the full leakage scale: a leaky flat round can be made exact on the same bath,
up to a constant, at a cost of the order of its leakage.

\begin{corollary}[Second-order rounding is false]\label{cor:second-order}
There are no constants $C_0,C_1$ such that every flat round of $\Phi_\omega$, $\theta=(\sqrt5-1)/2$, with distance at most $\frac1{16}$ and leakage
$\ell$ has a zero-leakage flat round with $S'\le S+C_0$, distance at most $\frac14$ and $a'\le a+C_1\ell\log(1/\ell)$.
\end{corollary}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:twisted}(b) with $\lambda=\gamma=1$. The witness is a twisted gadget, a gadget evaluated in the twisted group von Neumann
algebra of $\Z^2$; it has a flat probe, lies in the closure of finite tracial channels, and has the structure that the arguments of
Sections~\ref{sec:flat} and~\ref{sec:biased} use.

\subsection{Square fibres}

A round is \emph{square} if its slot operators are unitaries of one space. For leaky rounds of block-monomial form a rounding that keeps the monomial
pattern exists (it is part of the construction of Theorem~\ref{thm:BF}), but it cannot keep the fibres square.

\begin{proposition}[Square families cannot be rounded to square families]\label{prop:square}\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=1pt]
\item $\Phi_\omega$ has square flat rounds with $a=0$ and leakage tending to $0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:twisted}(b)), but no zero-leakage square round.
\item $\Phi_H$ has square flat rounds with $a=0$, distance at most $\frac1{16}$ and leakage tending to $0$, but no zero-leakage square round at distance below
$\frac12$. No gadget channel whose labels no finite-dimensional representation separates has a zero-leakage square round at distance below $\frac12$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

So a rounding of leaky rounds must in general leave the square class: the target for leaky block-monomial rounds is a family with rectangular
fibres, whose room is of order $\sqrt\ell$.

\subsection{The one-round method}

The lower bound $c\,n^{1/(2\log107+2)}$ for Houghton's channel comes from the one-round theorem~\cite[Theorem~5.5]{DouglasMghirbiB}, whose proof certifies $N$
approximately commuting copies of $S_3$ in the memory of one round and converts each into a sixteenth of a bit
(\cite[Appendix~B, proof of Theorem~5.5]{DouglasMghirbiB}). The certification needs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:amplification}
10^8\sqrt N\,\max\{N,A^*(3N)\}\,2^{17}\sqrt\nu\ \le\ 1,\qquad \nu=\ell+a,
\end{equation}
where $A^*(M)$ is the largest area of a commutator $[\sigma_p^{\pm1},\sigma_q^{\pm1}]$, $\sigma_j=\sfa^j\alpha\sfa^{-j}$, $0\le p,q\le M$, $|p-q|\ge2$, in
Johnson's presentation~\cite[Section~4.1]{DouglasMghirbiB}.

\begin{proposition}[The one-round method is capped at $n^{1/6}$]\label{prop:sixth}
Every $N\ge8$ that satisfies~\eqref{eq:amplification} has $N\le7.13\cdot10^{-6}\,\nu^{-1/5}$, whatever upper bound on $A^*$ is used. Consequently, combined
with Theorem~\ref{thm:extraction} and Proposition~\ref{prop:purity} at the rank rate, the method certifies $\Qs_{\Phi_H}(n,\epsilon)\ge Q_0$ only for
$Q_0=O(n^{1/6})$.
\end{proposition}

The cap comes from the quadratic lower bound on far-commutator areas~\cite[Proposition~4.4]{DouglasMghirbiB} together with the row sums of the
sector bound, which make $N$ copies cost $N^{5/2}$ in~\eqref{eq:amplification}. Lemma~\ref{lem:sector} removes the row sums when the copies
commute exactly, and Theorem~\ref{thm:genuine} then reaches $n^{1/3}$; for general devices a proof of the exponent $\frac13$ needs a sector bound that
is local in the same sense for copies that commute only approximately, or an argument that reads more than one round. The proofs of this section are
in Appendix~\ref{app:obstructions}.
